COLLECTION

RECONSTRUCTION

I begin organizing material relationally rather than chronologically. This is the first sequence in the chronology supported directly by the Index itself.

Source, interpretation, and reconstruction remain deliberately separated.

CONVERGENCE

Relationships involving capability, consequence, responsibility, maturity, trustworthiness, and autonomy appear across document classes. Their independence cannot always be established.

Convergence increases interest.

It does not resolve source.

PROSPECTIVE TEST

unresolved reference preserved

A condition appears in the retained repository state before the event I later interpret as its resolution.

I refuse to repair the missing destination.

STATE CHANGE

reference later resolves

The previously unavailable destination is later found to be reachable.

Directory, search, and canonical reconstruction remain inconsistent with the new state.

Whether this reflects intervention, an unnoticed repository change, or an error in my earlier inspection remains unresolved.

REASSESSMENT

reconstruction uncertainty introduced

I stop treating personal memory as a sufficient account of the Index's complete state. This is a change in my interpretation, not confirmation of another author.

External authorship is not assigned.

THRESHOLD

observation → interpretation → consequence → responsibility

The public reconstruction is arranged around a classification boundary.

In the present interface, additional material becomes available only after the relationship is interpreted rather than merely observed. That design condition should not be mistaken for evidence about the source of the material beyond it.
