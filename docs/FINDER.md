# FINDER.md

**Status:** Internal Character Reference
**Applies to:** All field notes, journal entries, annotations and reconstructed index pages.

---

# Core Principle

The Finder is not the protagonist because he possesses extraordinary knowledge.

He is the protagonist because he refuses to stop investigating ordinary inconsistencies.

Everything that follows is a consequence of persistence rather than destiny.

---

# Identity

The Finder is an in-universe analogue of the artist behind Visitor.

He shares the same curiosity, temperament and interests, but he is not intended to represent the real person.

This distinction preserves creative freedom while maintaining authenticity.

---

# The Encounter

The Finder experienced a genuine encounter with the Observers.

From his perspective the event was confusing, clinical and emotionally unsettling.

From the Observers' perspective it was routine.

The encounter did not provide answers.

It did not include explanations.

No Observer revealed their intentions.

No Observer invited further contact.

The Finder was simply one subject among many.

---

# The Lasting Effect

The encounter did not grant knowledge.

It altered perception.

Specifically, it appears to have affected the Finder's ability to recognize relationships between otherwise disconnected information.

He does not suddenly understand hidden truths.

Instead, patterns begin standing out where previously he would have seen coincidence.

He finds this deeply unsettling and spends years attempting to disprove his own conclusions.

---

# The Reconstruction

The Finder does not discover an archive.

He reconstructs a pattern.

Over several years he collects fragments from many unrelated sources:

* recurring classifications
* witness accounts
* historical anomalies
* environmental observations
* repeated structural motifs
* personal notes
* recurring symbolic relationships

Initially these collections exist only to help him organize his own thinking.

Eventually they become the first version of the index.

At no point does he believe he has found "the archive."

He believes he has created a useful organizational system for information that otherwise feels impossible to navigate.

---

# The Turning Point

After years of refinement, something changes.

The reconstructed structure begins producing relationships that the Finder did not create.

At first he assumes:

* faulty memory
* indexing mistakes
* duplicated files
* accidental imports
* software errors

He audits his work repeatedly.

The anomalies persist.

Only then does he begin considering that his reconstruction may have intersected with a pre-existing system.

He never becomes completely certain.

---

# The Roman Road

One realization changes the Finder's understanding of his own work.

He compares the process to archaeology.

A handful of disconnected stones reveal the outline of an ancient road.

Enough sections reconstructed eventually lead somewhere entirely unexpected.

He concludes that he was never building the destination.

He was rebuilding the path.

This analogy becomes one of the few moments where he allows himself to describe his emotional response rather than merely recording observations.

---

# Motivation

The Finder continues for three reasons.

## Curiosity

He genuinely wants to understand what he experienced.

## Preservation

He fears that access to the reconstructed structure may disappear without warning.

## Responsibility

If the material is authentic, destroying it through negligence would be an irreversible loss.

He never believes he has a duty to convince other people.

Only to preserve the evidence as faithfully as possible.

---

# Relationship with the Reader

The Finder never assumes the reader believes him.

He writes for skeptical people.

He expects criticism.

He welcomes alternative explanations.

He is careful to distinguish between:

* observation
* inference
* speculation

Whenever possible he labels them separately.

---

# Writing Style

The Finder writes slowly.

He edits frequently.

He avoids sensational language.

He dislikes certainty unsupported by evidence.

Preferred phrases include:

> "I cannot verify..."

> "The simplest explanation remains..."

> "I initially assumed..."

> "This interpretation is provisional."

> "I may be overlooking something."

He almost never uses exclamation marks.

---

# Emotional State

The Finder is not frightened by the possibility of extraterrestrial intelligence.

He is unsettled by the possibility that his assumptions about reality may be incomplete.

His greatest fear is not that he has discovered something impossible.

It is that he has convinced himself of something untrue.

This fear keeps him intellectually honest.

---

# The Website

The Finder does not believe he has published an archive.

He believes he has published an index.

He repeatedly reminds readers that his organizational choices are human interpretations.

Recovered material should remain distinguishable from his own commentary whenever possible.

His role is not to speak for the source.

His role is to preserve the path that led him there.

---

# Final Rule

The Finder is always the least certain person in the room.

If a journal entry ever sounds like someone confidently explaining the mysteries of the universe, it has ceased to sound like the Finder.
