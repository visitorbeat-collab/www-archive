DIRECTED PRESENCE TRANSMISSION 001

Deliberate population-originated signal directed toward an unidentified nonlocal recipient population.

SOURCE IDENTIFICATION

Arecibo Observatory radio transmission.

Local designation: Arecibo message.

Transmission epoch: 16 November 1974.

Designated direction: Messier 13 globular cluster.

HUMAN CLASSIFICATION

Demonstration of transmitter capability.

Interstellar message and ceremonial transmission.

EXTERNAL CLASSIFICATION

Directed presence transmission.

Active nonlocal projection of population identity under unverified recipient conditions.

OBSERVED FUNCTION

The originating population transmitted a structured radio signal away from its planetary system toward a dense and distant stellar population. The signal encoded mathematical, chemical, biological, spatial, and technological information selected to identify its source as artificial and to describe the population responsible for it.

Human accounts retain mixed emphasis concerning the transmission's purpose. It functioned as a demonstration of newly available transmitter power, but the signal itself was intentionally structured for interpretation by an intelligence not known to exist. Ceremonial intent does not remove communicative function.

RETAINED SIGNAL RECONSTRUCTION

Binary sequence arranged as 73 rows by 23 columns.

SIGNAL LENGTH: 1,679 binary units
FACTORIZATION: 23 × 73
TRANSMISSION FREQUENCY: 2,380 megahertz
TARGET DIRECTION: Messier 13
RECONSTRUCTION: monochrome / one cell per transmitted unit

RECONSTRUCTION NOTE

The transmission contained no colour, labels, section boundaries, or decoding instructions outside the sequence itself. Those elements are frequently added in later human diagrams and are not retained here.

The visual structure appears only after the sequence is divided according to the factors of its total length and assigned a two-dimensional orientation. Recognition therefore depends upon the recipient testing a relationship that was implied but never stated.

REPRESENTATIONAL CONTENT

The arranged sequence includes numerical relations, atomic identifiers associated with terrestrial biology, molecular descriptions, a representation of genetic structure, a human figure, a population value, the position of the world of origin within its planetary system, and a representation of the transmitting instrument.

The message joined a description of the population to a description of the mechanism by which that description had been sent. Source, body, location, and capability were presented as parts of the same identification event.

RECIPIENT CONDITION

No recipient had been detected before transmission. Recipient biology, sensory architecture, mathematics, symbolic practice, technical capacity, and interest in response were unknown. The population nevertheless selected relationships it expected another technical intelligence might independently reconstruct.

Successful interpretation required more than signal detection. A recipient would need to recognize artificial structure, preserve the sequence, identify its useful factorization, select an orientation, and infer that the resulting forms referred to conditions at the point of origin.

TEMPORAL CONDITION

The target region is sufficiently distant that any possible reception would occur long after the institutions and individuals responsible for the transmission had ceased to exist. A response following the same path could not return within the biological, political, or cultural continuity that authorized the initiating act.

The transmission therefore created a possible future relation without providing a mechanism through which its originators could participate in that relation. Intent was immediate. Consequence, if any, was deferred beyond available correction.

RETENTION BASIS

Earlier population artifacts could become legible if encountered. This event differed by directing a structured declaration outward rather than waiting for physical interception. Potential recognition was no longer attached only to the movement of an object.

The signal had little capacity to alter its target and an extremely low probability of producing exchange. It is retained because the population had begun to act upon the possibility of an unknown other before any reciprocal relationship existed.

ASSESSMENT NOTE

Presence can be asserted before relationship becomes possible.

The originating population disclosed identifying information without knowing whether the signal would be received, who might receive it, or what recognition might eventually produce. The practical risk of this instance remained low. The asymmetry it established is not dependent upon scale.

Increased transmission capability reduces the degree to which distance can function as containment. When a signal cannot be recalled and its possible recipient cannot be evaluated, the decision to transmit extends consequence beyond the population's ability to revise that decision.

INTERPRETIVE CAUTION

This record does not infer that the transmission was likely to be intercepted, decoded, or answered. It does not assign developmental significance to the technical power of the transmitter alone.

Relevance lies in the relationship between disclosure and control. The population performed an act whose intended meaning could outlast every structure capable of explaining, revising, or accepting responsibility for it.

The transmission was brief.

The declaration is still in transit.
