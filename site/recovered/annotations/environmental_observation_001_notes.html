environmental_observation_001_notes

related: environmental_observation_001 status: retained

I kept coming back to this file because I couldn't understand why it was there.

At first I treated it as environmental material and moved on.

That seemed reasonable.

The document appears to describe a decline in something roughly analogous to a pollinator population, followed by changes in plant reproduction, food availability, cultivated production, and finally a technological response.

None of that seemed especially relevant to the material I was trying to understand.

That assumption was probably the reason I missed the important part.

The subject keeps changing.

Or at least, I thought it did.

It begins with P-17.

Then plant reproduction.

Then the surrounding ecological network.

Then cultivated food production.

Then the response of a technological population.

Then back to the ecological network.

I would have separated those into different subjects.

Biology.
Agriculture.
Technology.
Population behavior.
Environmental change.

Whoever produced this record did not.

The document treats them as successive states of the same event.

That distinction matters.

I reread classification_schema_v1 after noticing this.

The primary observational unit is the interacting system.

I think I misunderstood what that meant.

I assumed it was simply a preference for broader context.

I am no longer sure that is sufficient.

The categories I have been using may be introducing divisions that are not present in the source material.

I have spent years asking what individual documents are about.

That may be the wrong question.

The better question may be:

What process is this document observing?

If that interpretation is correct, then environmental_observation_001 is not primarily about P-17.

P-17 is where the event became visible.

The event itself is larger.

I don't think they saw separate subjects.

I think they saw one event.

I am leaving the original environmental classification in place for now.

It is useful as a record of how I initially understood the file.
