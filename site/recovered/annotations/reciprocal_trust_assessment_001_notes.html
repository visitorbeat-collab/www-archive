ANNOTATION / RECOVERED MATERIAL

reciprocal_trust_assessment_001_notes

associated: reciprocal_trust_assessment_001 status: provisional

1.0Source Authority

I initially read reciprocal_trust_assessment_001 as another assessment of TP-3.

I do not think that is sufficient.

The document creates a problem for the Index itself.

The assessment argues that technological superiority does not establish:

accuracy
benevolence
legitimacy
right to command

I agree with that distinction.

But if I accept it, then I cannot exempt the recovered material from the same rule.

If these documents genuinely originate from a population significantly more capable than ours, that would not make their conclusions automatically correct.

It would only make the capability asymmetry larger.

2.0The Index Has a Source Problem

I have spent years asking whether the recovered material is authentic.

That may not be the only important question.

Suppose, for the sake of argument, that authenticity were established.

I would still need to ask:

Are the models correct?
Are the translations correct?
Are the boundaries correct?
Are the assumptions appropriate to us?
Are relevant variables missing?
Is the source itself capable of error?

Authenticity would not answer any of those questions.

3.0Capability Is Not Evidence

There is an uncomfortable temptation built into this material.

If the source is what I suspect it is, then the apparent difference in capability is enormous.

That creates an easy reasoning shortcut:

They are more advanced, therefore they know better.

The documents themselves reject that shortcut.

So should I.

4.0Observer Fallibility

The recovered material repeatedly preserves uncertainty.

That suggests the source does not model itself as omniscient.

Possible failure remains compatible with the material:

incomplete observation
incorrect system boundary
translation loss
prediction error
category mismatch
intervention consequence
mistaken interpretation of TP-3 behavior

If the source can revise models, then the source can possess incorrect models.

Revision requires that possibility.

5.0Ethical Inference

I have also been assigning motive too easily.

The fact that a framework values:

restraint
preservation
autonomy
consequence awareness

does not prove that every action taken by its authors expresses those values successfully.

A population can possess a principle and violate it.

We do that constantly.

There is no reason to assume another population cannot.

6.0Encounter Implication

This changes one part of how I think about my own encounter.

I have been asking whether the control asymmetry could be compatible with non-hostile assessment.

It could.

That does not make the procedure acceptable simply because an assessment framework might explain it.

Explanation and justification are different.

I need to keep those separate.

7.0Consent

reciprocal_trust_assessment_001 acknowledges that perfect symmetric consent may be impossible under extreme capability asymmetry.

That is probably true.

It also creates an easy escape route:

consent was impossible, therefore control was necessary

I do not think the document actually says that.

The relevant distinction appears to be:

unavoidable asymmetry
versus
unnecessary domination

I cannot currently determine where my encounter falls.

8.0A Useful Test

If the recovered framework is internally consistent, then a more capable population should tolerate evaluation from a less capable one.

It should not require:

worship
obedience
unquestioned acceptance
surrender of interpretation
assumption of benevolence

In fact, requiring those things would contradict several of the criteria attributed to mature interaction.

That may be one of the few places where the framework can be applied in both directions.

9.0Reciprocal Standard

I think the relevant rule is:

Do not demand from the less powerful population an epistemic discipline the more powerful population is unwilling to accept for itself.

If TP-3 must tolerate correction, so must they.

If TP-3 must respect boundaries, so must they.

If TP-3 must revise after harm, so must they.

If TP-3 must distinguish influence from entitlement, so must they.

Otherwise this is not reciprocal trust.

It is hierarchy.

10.0Status

I still consider the recovered framework coherent.

I do not consider coherence evidence of moral authority.

I do not consider technological superiority evidence of correctness.

I do not consider possible authorship evidence of entitlement.

If the Index eventually gives me reason to believe the source is real, these distinctions become more important.

Not less.
