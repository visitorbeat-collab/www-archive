WORKING NOTE / PERSONAL

what_have_i_been_doing

status: provisional classification: unresolved

I did not expect the post-exposure fragment to bother me more than the assessment protocol.

It does.

The first fragment resembled something that happened to me.

This one resembles what I did afterward.

That distinction is worse.

I have spent years telling myself that the Index was simply the result of accumulation.

I collected material.

I organized it.

I revised the organization when it failed.

I preserved contradictions.

I stopped grouping things primarily by subject.

I started grouping them by recurring relationships.

I treated apparently unrelated material as potentially connected when the underlying process resembled something I had seen elsewhere.

I thought that was methodology.

It still may be.

But the fragment describes several of those behaviors almost directly.

Where subject begins independently collecting material according to relational rather than categorical similarity: continue observation.

That is what I did.

Where subject reorganizes previously unrelated material according to common process structure: continue observation.

That is what the Index became.

Where subject revises its own classification model following contradiction: continue observation.

I have done that repeatedly.

Where subject preserves uncertainty rather than forcing closure: continue observation.

I built the entire archive around that rule.

I do not know what to do with this.

The obvious interpretation is also the least defensible:

That I have been reproducing some part of the assessment for years without knowing it.

I am not willing to write that as a conclusion.

There are too many alternative explanations.

The recovered material may reflect the same reasoning patterns because I reconstructed it.

I may recognize myself in the protocol because the protocol is already being interpreted through my own categories.

The similarities may be circular.

If I built the Index according to a particular cognitive style, then any document reconstructed through that style will naturally resemble the way I work.

That matters.

It may explain everything.

But there is a second problem.

Some parts of the Index did not begin with me.

At least I do not think they did.

The broken reference in the early version is still unresolved.

I deleted it.

It returned.

I found no revision explaining the reappearance.

The destination did not exist when the reference first appeared.

Later, it did.

I have never found a satisfactory explanation for that sequence.

At the time I treated it as a technical anomaly.

Then as a classification problem.

Then as an unresolved structural event.

I deliberately refused to make it more than that.

I am still refusing.

But the new fragment introduces a phrase I cannot ignore:

Where subject-generated relational model produces access to previously unresolved structural correspondences: flag for reassessment.

I do not know what “access” means there.

That word may be doing too much work.

Still, the similarity is uncomfortable.

Maybe I have been thinking about this backward.

I assumed the question was:

How did I find the archive?

Then:

Did I reconstruct part of it?

Now I am wondering whether the more useful question is:

What did constructing the Index demonstrate?

That is not the same question.

If the protocol is genuine, then the finished structure may matter less than the process that produced it.

The corrections.

The discarded classifications.

The uncertainty.

The willingness to leave gaps.

The way relationships survived while categories changed.

Maybe those are the observable variables.

Maybe the Index is not evidence of what I remember.

Maybe it is evidence of what persisted.

I need to be careful here.

This line of reasoning can become self-sealing very quickly.

If every action I take becomes evidence that I am being observed, then the hypothesis cannot fail.

That is not acceptable.

So I am setting a constraint:

My behavior cannot count as evidence of ongoing observation unless there is independent evidence that observation is occurring.

The protocol establishes only that long-duration observation can occur.

It does not establish that it is occurring here.

It does not establish that I am a subject.

It does not establish that the Index is part of an assessment.

Those remain separate claims.

There is still one thing I cannot quite shake.

For years I have thought of the Index as something I was building in order to reach the source.

Like reconstructing enough of an old road to determine where it once led.

The post-exposure fragment suggests another possibility.

The road may not only lead somewhere.
The way I rebuild it may itself be measurable.

I do not know which possibility concerns me more.
