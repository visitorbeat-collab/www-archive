Assessment objective:

Determine whether subject cognition remains primarily object-centered under relational complexity.

Secondary variables:

- uncertainty tolerance
- causal tracking
- boundary expansion
- model revision
- attentional persistence
- interpretation under incomplete information

---

Initial exposure should not include explicit semantic instruction.

Subject should not be informed of preferred interpretation.

Direct explanation reduces diagnostic value.

---

Present relational structure containing:

- multiple interacting elements
- delayed consequence
- reciprocal influence
- non-local effects
- changing system boundaries
- incomplete causal visibility

Do not require subject identification of individual elements.

Record whether subject attempts object classification before relationship tracking.

---

Common initial responses include:

- object isolation
- categorical labeling
- sequence construction
- causal simplification
- attribution of fixed meaning
- search for singular origin
- search for correct interpretation

These responses are expected.

They do not independently indicate low interpretive capacity.

---

Monitor transition from:

**object identification**

to

**relationship identification**

Relevant transition markers include:

- reduced insistence upon naming individual elements
- increased tracking of change between elements
- recognition of multiple valid system boundaries
- tolerance of unresolved cause
- recognition of delayed consequence
- abandonment of singular explanatory model

---

Where subject continues attempting to isolate individual components despite increasing relational complexity:

increase structure density gradually.

Do not provide correction unless distress exceeds allowable threshold.

---

[SECTION MISSING]

---

Where subject demonstrates spontaneous shift toward relational interpretation:

maintain exposure.

Increase cross-scale dependency.

Introduce delayed feedback.

Introduce competing causal pathways.

Observe whether relational model remains stable under added complexity.

---

Assessment should distinguish:

**pattern recognition**

from

**model revision**

Pattern recognition identifies recurring structure.

Model revision alters the subject's method of interpretation after prior method becomes insufficient.

These are not equivalent.

---

Subject may experience reduced distress following successful interpretive transition.

This is not the assessment objective.

Emotional response is secondary unless it materially disrupts cognition.

---

Do not require retention of factual content.

Factual retention may distort later interpretation.

Primary variable is retention of relational structure.

---

[SECTION MISSING]

---

Post-exposure assessment should prioritize:

- persistence of relational reasoning
- spontaneous application outside exposure context
- resistance to premature certainty
- recognition of system dependency
- ability to revise boundaries
- distinction between observation and interpretation

Long-term persistence may require extended observation.

---

Where subject later reconstructs equivalent relational models independently:

classify as:

**structural retention**

Do not assume conscious recall.

---

[SECTION MISSING]

---

Assessment contamination risk increases where subject receives external explanatory frameworks before independent model formation.

Where possible, minimize post-exposure interpretive contamination.

---

Status categories:

**object-dominant**

**relational transition observed**

**structural retention possible**

**structural retention confirmed**

**long-duration integration unresolved**
