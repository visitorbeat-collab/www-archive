# Working Note

I keep returning to the same problem.

If these records are assessments, what would make the assessment complete?

Intelligence does not seem sufficient.

Capability does not seem sufficient.

Technical success does not seem sufficient.

Even correction after failure does not seem sufficient by itself.

The observations continue.

That suggests the standard is not achievement.

It may be predictability.

I do not mean prediction in the narrow technical sense.

I mean whether a population can be expected to regulate its own influence when the conditions become difficult.

When consequence is delayed.

When uncertainty remains.

When restraint is expensive.

When no one is watching.

When the affected system cannot object.

When the benefit is immediate and the damage belongs to someone else.

When the damage belongs to the future.

A population that behaves responsibly only while consequences are visible is difficult to predict.

A population that revises only after catastrophe is difficult to predict.

A population that understands dependency but abandons maintenance when it becomes inconvenient is difficult to predict.

Maybe that is the point.

Not:

**Are they intelligent?**

Not:

**Are they moral?**

Not even:

**Will they make mistakes?**

Maybe:

**Can increasing influence be trusted to remain coupled to self-regulation?**

I do not know whether "trust" is the right word.

It is probably too human.

Trust implies intention.

Relationship.

Expectation.

Maybe the underlying concept is something closer to:

**reliable behavioral constraint under expanding capability**

That is ugly, but it may be more precise.

Still, I cannot stop thinking about contact.

If a more advanced civilization had access to knowledge or technology that could dramatically increase our capabilities, the obvious danger would not be that we were unintelligent.

The danger would be giving more influence to a population whose internal limits did not scale with that influence.

That would explain the long observation periods.

You could not measure that in a single encounter.

You could not measure it during a crisis alone.

You would have to watch what happens afterward.

Then later.

Then after the memory fades.

You would have to see whether revised behavior survives changing incentives.

You would have to see whether restraint survives opportunity.

You would have to see whether systems outside immediate human concern are eventually incorporated into the boundary of concern.

That takes time.

A lot of time.

Maybe the question is not whether we deserve contact.

I dislike that framing more every time I write it.

Maybe the question is whether contact would increase instability.

And if that is true, then the thing being measured may not be worthiness at all.

It may be trustworthiness.

I am leaving that word provisional.

I do not yet know what would count as evidence for or against it.

But it explains more of the structure than "intelligence" does.

# Important Distinction

Trust does not mean:

- obedience
- moral purity
- lack of error
- agreement with Observer values
- technological inferiority
- submission

Trust means something closer to:

> confidence that increasing influence will remain constrained by persistent self-correction, appropriate restraint, and awareness of consequence.

This should remain mostly internal for now.

---

# Why Trust Matters for Contact

Contact may increase:

- knowledge
- technological capability
- coordination capacity
- access to other systems
- access to other populations
- consequence scale

The Observers therefore cannot evaluate contact only by asking:

> Can TP-3 understand what is being offered?

They must also ask:

> How will expanded capability be used after it is understood?

This makes trustworthiness structurally relevant.

---

# Long Observation Period

Trustworthiness cannot be inferred from isolated good decisions.

The Observers need to observe:

- response to failure
- persistence of revision
- behavior under uncertainty
- restraint under opportunity
- long-horizon maintenance
- treatment of unrepresented populations
- behavior when consequence is delayed

This explains the long duration of observation.

A population that recognizes dependency only intermittently remains unstable.

A population that incorporates dependency into durable behavior becomes easier to model.

# Relationship to Responsibility

Responsibility remains a candidate human interpretation.

Trustworthiness is different.

Responsibility describes the internal relationship between influence and behavior.

Trustworthiness describes how another population might evaluate the reliability of that relationship.

    ↓
CONSEQUENCE
