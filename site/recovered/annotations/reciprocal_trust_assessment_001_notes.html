reciprocal_trust_assessment_001_notes

associated: reciprocal_trust_assessment_001 status: provisional

1.0Source Authority

When I first read reciprocal_trust_assessment_001 , I treated it mostly as another assessment of TP-3.

I think that misses one of the more uncomfortable implications of the document.

The assessment argues that technological superiority does not automatically establish accuracy, benevolence, legitimacy, or a right to command.

I agree with that.

The problem is that if I actually believe it, I have to apply the same standard to the recovered material itself.

If these documents really do come from a population vastly more capable than ours, that would tell me something extraordinary about their origin.

It would not make every conclusion in them correct.

It would mostly make the difference in capability harder to ignore.

2.0The Index Has a Source Problem

I have spent years worrying about whether the recovered material is authentic.

I'm starting to think I have given that question too much weight.

Suppose for a moment that authenticity could somehow be established beyond reasonable doubt.

I would still have to ask whether the models are accurate, whether the translations are reliable, whether the boundaries have been drawn correctly, whether important variables are missing, and whether assumptions that make sense to the source also make sense when applied to us.

I would also have to allow for the possibility that the source itself is simply wrong about something.

Authenticity would answer none of those questions.

I don't think I had separated those problems clearly enough before.

3.0Capability Is Not Evidence

There is a reasoning shortcut here that I can feel myself wanting to take.

If the source is what I suspect it may be, then the difference in capability between us is difficult to overstate.

From there it is very easy to move to:

They are more advanced, therefore they know better.

Sometimes they probably would know better.

That still doesn't make the inference safe as a general rule.

More importantly, the documents themselves seem to reject it.

I can't use the framework to criticize deference to authority and then exempt its authors from the same criticism.

4.0Observer Fallibility

The recovered material preserves uncertainty often enough that I don't think the source models itself as omniscient.

There are repeated allowances for incomplete observation, incorrect system boundaries, translation loss, prediction error, category mismatch, unintended consequences of intervention, and mistaken interpretation of TP-3 behavior.

That matters.

A system that revises its models has to allow for the possibility that its existing models are wrong.

Revision would be meaningless otherwise.

Whatever level of capability is represented here, fallibility seems to remain part of the framework.

5.0Ethical Inference

I think I have also been assigning motive too easily.

The framework appears to value restraint, preservation, autonomy, and awareness of consequence.

I find those values sympathetic, which is probably part of the problem.

A population can hold a principle and still fail to live according to it.

Humans manage that without much difficulty.

I don't see a good reason to assume another population would be immune to the same gap between principle and behavior.

So even if I have reconstructed their values correctly, I can't work backward from those values and assume every action attributed to them was justified.

6.0Encounter Implication

This changes something about how I think about my own encounter.

I have spent a lot of time asking whether the control asymmetry I remember could be compatible with some kind of non-hostile assessment.

I think it could be.

But that only gives me a possible explanation for the procedure.

It doesn't tell me the procedure was acceptable.

Explanation and justification are different.

That distinction seems obvious written down.

I'm not sure I was applying it consistently before.

7.0Consent

reciprocal_trust_assessment_001 acknowledges that perfectly symmetrical consent may be impossible when the difference in capability is extreme.

That seems plausible to me.

It also creates a very convenient argument if handled badly:

consent was impossible, therefore control was necessary

I don't think the document actually supports that conclusion.

The more useful distinction seems to be between asymmetry that cannot realistically be removed and domination that was not necessary to the interaction.

Those are harder to separate than I would like.

I still can't say where my own encounter falls.

8.0A Useful Test

There is at least one implication of the framework that seems testable in principle.

If a more capable population genuinely values independent evaluation, then it should be able to tolerate being evaluated by a less capable one.

It should not require worship, obedience, unquestioned acceptance, surrender of interpretation, or an assumption of benevolence before evidence is available.

Requiring those things would contradict too much of the framework's own language about maturity and autonomy.

This may be one of the few standards in the Index that can be applied in both directions without much modification.

9.0Reciprocal Standard

The rule I am using for now is:

Do not demand from the less powerful population an epistemic discipline the more powerful population is unwilling to accept for itself.

If TP-3 is expected to tolerate correction, then the external population has to tolerate correction too.

The same goes for respecting boundaries, revising after harm, and distinguishing influence from entitlement.

I don't expect symmetry of capability.

I'm not even sure symmetry of obligation is possible in every situation.

But if all of the obligations flow in one direction, I have difficulty calling the resulting relationship reciprocal.

At that point it starts looking more like hierarchy.

10.0Status

I still think the recovered framework is internally coherent.

I also think I need to be much more careful about what I infer from that coherence.

It doesn't give the source moral authority, and superior technology would not make every claim correct.

Even establishing authorship, if that ever becomes possible, would tell me who produced the material rather than what authority I should grant them.

If the Index eventually gives me stronger reason to believe the source is real, these distinctions will matter more than they do now.

I don't want certainty about the source to become the point where I stop evaluating it.
