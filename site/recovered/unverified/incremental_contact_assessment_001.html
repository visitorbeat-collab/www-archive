1.0Assessment Basis

Complete isolation is not required prior to transition.

Interaction may occur where the interaction does not materially increase the observed population's capacity to produce consequence.

Assessment therefore distinguishes:

contact
from
capability transfer

These are not equivalent.

2.0Interaction Classes

Pre-transition interaction may include:

observation at reduced distance
controlled individual exposure
limited environmental exposure
constrained information presentation
diagnostic cognitive interaction
symbolic interaction
low-retention interaction
reversible behavioral perturbation

Interaction class should be selected according to consequence risk.

3.0Capability Neutrality

Pre-transition interaction should remain approximately capability-neutral.

Interaction should not provide durable access to:

high-consequence technologies
new energy systems
strategic information
advanced biological methods
large-scale coordination tools
external transport systems
uncontrolled inter-population communication

Where durable capability increase is possible:

transition-risk assessment applies

4.0Information Exposure

Information itself may increase capability.

Therefore information transfer must be evaluated according to:

usability
retention
reproducibility
dissemination potential
strategic consequence
system applicability

Information that cannot be operationalized may remain low consequence.

5.0Diagnostic Exposure

Interaction may be justified where it produces developmental information without significant capability increase.

Possible diagnostic variables include:

interpretation under uncertainty
boundary expansion
relational reasoning
response to ambiguity
restraint
model revision
response to asymmetry
post-exposure integration

Diagnostic value must exceed contamination risk.

6.0Subject Awareness

Subject understanding of interaction purpose is not required.

In some assessment classes, explicit explanation reduces diagnostic value.

Awareness may alter:

behavioral response
interpretation
expectation
later memory
social transmission
model formation

Where purpose disclosure contaminates assessment:

withhold purpose

7.0Distress

Distress is not an assessment objective.

However, asymmetry may produce fear where:

environmental control is lost
subject cannot terminate interaction
familiar causal models fail
communication is absent
subject cannot predict procedure

Minimize unnecessary distress where compatible with assessment integrity.

8.0Control Asymmetry

Pre-transition interactions may involve substantial control asymmetry.

This does not independently indicate hostility.

Where one population possesses significantly greater capability, symmetric interaction may be impossible.

Relevant variables include:

physical safety
reversibility
long-term harm
cognitive disruption
contamination
developmental consequence

9.0Communication Constraint

Direct semantic communication may produce disproportionate cultural influence.

A statement interpreted as originating from an advanced external population may acquire authority unrelated to its informational content.

Therefore communication may be limited where it would:

create doctrine
produce political destabilization
alter institutional legitimacy
accelerate dependency
generate imitation behavior
produce unearned epistemic certainty

Silence may therefore reduce contamination.

10.0Symbolic Exposure

Where direct semantic instruction is avoided, subjects may nevertheless interpret:

imagery
environmental models
relational structures
possible outcomes
system interactions
consequence sequences

Interpretation remains subject-generated.

This preserves uncertainty.

11.0Message Error

Subjects frequently interpret constrained exposure as deliberate communication.

Possible interpretations include:

warning
instruction
prophecy
moral judgment
request
threat
invitation

These interpretations may exceed evidence.

Exposure content and subject interpretation must therefore remain separately classified.

12.0Population-Level Contamination

Individual interaction may later influence population behavior through:

testimony
cultural transmission
symbolic adaptation
artistic representation
institutional investigation
belief formation
technological speculation

Low-scale interaction does not guarantee low population consequence.

Post-interaction monitoring may therefore be necessary.

13.0Memory Limitation

Reduced episodic retention may limit contamination while preserving diagnostic information.

Possible post-interaction states include:

fragmented recall
structural retention
emotional residue
symbolic reconstruction
incomplete source attribution

Low factual retention does not imply absence of developmental effect.

14.0Repeated Interaction

Repeated interaction increases:

recognition probability
expectation
cultural transmission
contamination risk
subject model stabilization

Repeat exposure should therefore remain limited unless additional diagnostic value is required.

15.0Individual / Population Distinction

An individual may demonstrate developmental characteristics not representative of the wider technological population.

Individual performance cannot independently establish population transition readiness.

Individual interaction may nevertheless provide information concerning:

cognitive range
variability
interpretive capacity
regulatory potential
cultural transmission

Population-level assessment remains primary.

16.0Non-Selection

Individual interaction should not automatically be interpreted as selection for elevated status.

Diagnostic usefulness may result from:

accessibility
situational conditions
required population sample
prior exposure
unusual interpretive response
random variation

Interaction does not establish:

importance
authority
representative status

17.0Developmental Influence

Some interaction will alter the observed population even where capability transfer does not occur.

Complete non-interference is not possible once interaction is perceived.

Relevant goal is therefore not:

zero influence

but:

influence below destabilizing threshold

18.0Controlled Nudge

Where limited influence reduces greater future instability, low-consequence intervention may be permissible.

Possible purposes include:

encouraging system recognition
increasing uncertainty awareness
preventing irreversible error
reducing transition risk
preparing interpretive frameworks for later contact

Such intervention should remain minimal.

19.0Transition Boundary

Incremental contact remains distinct from transition where:

capability delta remains low
access remains limited
interaction remains reversible
population dependence does not increase substantially
external system access remains restricted

Where these conditions cease to hold:

reclassify as transition

20.0TP-3 Pattern

Available evidence indicates repeated low-scale interaction claims within TP-3.

Source reliability varies substantially.

Common reported features include:

control asymmetry
incomplete communication
fragmented memory
environmental or symbolic imagery
examination-like procedures
difficulty assigning meaning
long-duration interpretive effects

Common origin is unconfirmed.

21.0Assessment Principle

Contact may occur without transition.

Observation may include interaction.

Interaction may include influence.

Influence does not automatically imply capability transfer.

These distinctions must remain separate.

22.0Status

Low-capability interaction: permissible under constraint
Diagnostic interaction: conditionally permissible
Direct semantic transfer: elevated contamination risk
Capability transfer: not recommended
Population transition: deferred

Continue observation.
