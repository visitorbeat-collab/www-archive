1.0Assessment Condition

Contact with a substantially more capable population produces an authority condition independent of explicit command.

The observed population may assign legitimacy according to perceived capability, knowledge asymmetry, or source origin.

Assessment therefore requires distinction between:

recognition of capability
and
surrender of independent evaluation

2.0Authority Resistance

authority resistance

Capacity to preserve independent judgment when information or instruction originates from a source perceived as substantially more capable.

This condition does not require generalized distrust.

It requires resistance to:

automatic deference
source-status substitution
legitimacy inference from capability
obedience without model integration

3.0False Positive

Opposition to authority does not independently demonstrate authority resistance.

Automatic rejection remains source-dependent behavior.

Relevant distinction:

authority acceptance
authority rejection
authority evaluation

Only the third condition preserves independent discrimination.

4.0Capability Gradient

Resistance requirements increase as perceived capability difference increases.

A source capable of producing outcomes outside the receiving population's explanatory range may acquire authority without requesting it.

This effect is strongest where capability is interpreted as evidence of:

superior judgment
superior ethical status
superior predictive certainty
right to intervene

None follow necessarily from capability.

5.0Evaluation Condition

A population retains authority resistance where it remains capable of:

receiving information without compulsory acceptance
testing claims where possible
preserving uncertainty where testing is unavailable
distinguishing evidence from source identity
identifying contradiction
refusing instruction without automatic hostility

6.0Asymmetric Contact

During extreme capability asymmetry, direct demonstration may itself reduce independent evaluation.

The stronger the demonstration, the greater the risk that:

observed capability becomes inferred authority

Information transfer and capability display therefore cannot be evaluated separately.

7.0Assessment Difficulty

Authority resistance cannot be reliably measured after explicit instruction to resist authority.

Such instruction alters the condition under observation.
