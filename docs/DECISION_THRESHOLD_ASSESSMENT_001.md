# 1.0 Assessment Condition

TP-3 possesses increasing capacity to initiate modifications whose complete effects cannot be known prior to intervention.

This condition is not anomalous.

Prediction remains incomplete for all observed populations.

The relevant assessment variable is therefore not elimination of uncertainty.

It is the amount of unresolved uncertainty accepted before consequential intervention proceeds.

---

# 2.0 Decision Threshold

For present assessment:

**decision threshold**

is defined as the minimum evidentiary condition required before an intervention is permitted to proceed.

Threshold may incorporate:

- predicted benefit
- predicted harm
- uncertainty
- reversibility
- intervention scale
- affected system size
- recovery capacity
- consequence duration
- availability of lower-impact alternatives

Thresholds vary substantially across TP-3 systems.

---

# 3.0 Low-Consequence Intervention

Where possible consequence is:

- localized
- temporary
- highly reversible
- well observed

TP-3 frequently accepts substantial uncertainty.

This produces limited developmental concern.

Prediction failure within these conditions is usually recoverable.

Experimentation may increase future model accuracy.

---

# 4.0 Increasing Consequence

As potential consequence increases:

- affected population size may increase
- affected system boundaries may expand
- recovery interval may increase
- reversibility may decrease
- effects may persist beyond initiating populations
- consequence may outlast the decision-makers responsible for initiation

Under these conditions, prediction error becomes increasingly significant.

---

# 5.0 Threshold Relationship

Observed stable decision systems commonly demonstrate the following relationship:

**potential consequence increases**

→

**required confidence increases**

→

**accepted uncertainty decreases**

This relationship is not absolute.

Delay itself may produce consequence.

Non-intervention may also produce consequence.

Assessment therefore includes comparative risk.

---

# 6.0 Non-Action Is Not Neutral

Absence of intervention does not necessarily preserve existing conditions.

Observed systems may continue changing without deliberate action.

Decision analysis must therefore compare:

**consequence of intervention**

with

**consequence of non-intervention**

rather than:

**action**

with

**no consequence**

Failure to represent this distinction produces distorted threshold assessment.

---

# 7.0 Reversibility Weighting

Uncertainty carries different significance depending upon reversibility.

Equivalent predictive uncertainty may be tolerated where:

**effects are reversible**

and rejected where:

**effects are irreversible**

Observed relationship:

**uncertainty × irreversibility = increased decision significance**

Exact mathematical representation is not implied.

Relationship is functional.

---

# 8.0 Scale Weighting

Equivalent uncertainty also produces different developmental significance across unequal scales.

A prediction error affecting one local system is not equivalent to the same prediction error affecting:

- multiple ecosystems
- distributed populations
- long-duration resource systems
- inherited biological conditions
- atmospheric systems
- planetary-scale networks

Assessment must include consequence boundary.

---

# 9.0 TP-3 Pattern

TP-3 demonstrates significant capacity to raise decision thresholds in response to:

- visible risk
- immediate consequence
- high reversibility cost
- concentrated accountability
- recent system failure

Threshold elevation is less consistent where:

- consequence is delayed
- consequence is distributed
- causal attribution is difficult
- benefit is immediate
- affected populations are distant
- damage accumulates gradually
- intervention is culturally normalized

---

# 10.0 Capability Interaction

Increasing technical capability expands the number of interventions available to TP-3.

It also expands the number of interventions whose effects may exceed existing predictive models.

Therefore:

**capability expansion without corresponding threshold adjustment increases developmental instability**

Capability itself is not the unstable variable.

The relationship between capability and decision standard is the relevant variable.

---

# 11.0 Comparative Assessment

Two populations possess equivalent intervention capability.

Population A requires low confidence before high-consequence intervention.

Population B requires higher confidence, greater reversibility, or reduced-scale testing.

Both populations may achieve similar technical outcomes during ordinary conditions.

Their developmental risk differs.

Observed difference is not intelligence.

Observed difference is behavioral regulation of influence under uncertainty.

---

# 12.0 Threshold Failure

Threshold failure may occur when:

- expected benefit is overweighted
- uncertainty is excluded
- affected systems fall outside the decision boundary
- reversibility is assumed rather than demonstrated
- delayed consequence is discounted
- low-frequency outcomes are ignored
- prior success is generalized beyond relevant conditions

Threshold failure may occur despite technically accurate reasoning within an incomplete model.

---

# 13.0 Developmental Significance

As the magnitude of possible influence increases, the consequences of insufficient decision thresholds increase.

The required standard for action therefore cannot remain constant across increasing capability.

Observed principle:

**greater possible consequence requires greater decision discrimination**

This does not require certainty.

It requires proportional adjustment.

---

# 14.0 Assessment Note

TP-3 demonstrates awareness of this relationship in multiple domains.

Application remains inconsistent.

The population frequently increases evidentiary requirements where possible consequences are immediate and legible.

Equivalent adjustment is less reliable where consequence is delayed, distributed, cumulative, or difficult to reverse.

This distinction remains developmentally significant.

# Important Nuance

The Observers must not imply that non-action is automatically superior.

Section 6 is essential.

A sophisticated decision framework must recognize that:

- action has consequences
- delay has consequences
- non-action has consequences

The developmental question is whether these consequences are represented proportionally.

This prevents the archive from becoming a simplistic argument for technological caution.

---

# Relationship to Responsibility

Responsibility begins emerging here as proportionality.

The eventual concept should feel something like:

> The standard governing the use of influence should scale with the consequence that influence can produce.

This is much stronger than:

> Be careful.

It makes responsibility structurally necessary rather than culturally moralistic.

---

# Relationship to Trust

Internal only.

A population whose decision thresholds scale predictably with consequence is easier for another civilization to trust.

A population possessing high capability but inconsistent thresholds is difficult to predict.

Trust will eventually emerge not from goodness but from reliable self-regulation.
