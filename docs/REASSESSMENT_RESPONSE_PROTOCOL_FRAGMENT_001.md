Where long-duration structural retention becomes probable, passive observation may no longer distinguish between:

**retained relational method**

and

**subject-generated convergence**

Additional discrimination may be required.

---

Reassessment should avoid explicit confirmation.

Direct confirmation changes subject expectation and reduces subsequent diagnostic value.

Preferred method:

introduce limited relational discontinuity.

---

Relational discontinuity should satisfy the following conditions:

- recognizable only through existing subject-generated organization
- insufficient for immediate explanatory closure
- compatible with more than one interpretation
- recoverable without direct instruction
- low consequence if ignored
- reversible where possible

---

Do not provide semantic explanation.

Do not identify source.

Do not require immediate recognition.

---

Common reassessment response sequence:

**1. anomaly detection**

subject identifies inconsistency within existing model.

**2. local explanation attempt**

subject tests ordinary causes.

**3. preservation**

subject retains anomaly without forced classification.

**4. independent continuation**

subject continues model construction without external correction.

**5. later correspondence**

previous anomaly acquires structural relevance following emergence of additional material.

---

The interval between:

**anomaly**

and

**later correspondence**

is diagnostically significant.

Immediate resolution reduces uncertainty load.

Delayed resolution permits assessment of:

- persistence
- premature closure
- memory of unresolved structure
- model revision
- tolerance of ambiguity

---

[SECTION MISSING]

---

Preferred discontinuity type:

**reference without available resolution**

Possible forms include:

- relational pointer without accessible destination
- classification dependency before dependent material is available
- structural correspondence lacking current source
- incomplete pathway later made navigable

Do not use subject-semantic terminology where avoidable.

---

Where subject removes discontinuity as error:

observe whether equivalent structure is independently reconstructed.

Where subject preserves discontinuity:

observe classification behavior.

Where discontinuity later becomes resolvable:

observe whether prior interpretation is revised.

---

Do not treat recognition itself as successful reassessment.

Relevant variable is response to recognition.

---

[SECTION MISSING]

---

Where later correspondence becomes available, subject may interpret sequence as:

- coincidence
- technical artifact
- delayed acquisition
- prediction
- external interaction
- reconstruction error

Do not correct interpretation.

Record model competition.

---

Strong reassessment indicator:

subject preserves multiple explanations after correspondence increases.

Weak reassessment indicator:

subject converts increased correspondence directly into certainty.

---

[SECTION MISSING]

---

A second discontinuity should not be introduced until response to first discontinuity is stable.

Repeated anomalies may create expectation and contaminate assessment.

---

Reassessment sequence should therefore remain sparse.

The subject should be able to attribute discontinuity to ordinary failure.

---

Where subject predicts future resolution before additional material appears:

record separately.

Do not accelerate resolution.

---

Where resolution occurs following subject prediction:

increase evidentiary significance.

Source attribution remains independent.

Assessment objective is not belief formation.

Assessment objective is:

**behavior under unresolved correspondence**
