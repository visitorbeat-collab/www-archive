0001 / FIELD NOTE

Why This Exists

created: various revised: various status: retained

If you're reading these notes, you've already put more effort into this than I ever expected anyone else to.

I think that matters, although probably not for the reason it first sounds like.

There isn't exactly a shortage of information anymore. The problem is that almost everything is competing to interrupt whatever came before it. Following something slowly, especially when there isn't an obvious reward at the end, has become surprisingly difficult.

So if you've made it this far, you've probably also noticed that I'm careful about what I claim.

That's deliberate.

I have no interest in convincing anyone that something extraordinary happened to me. For a long time I was much more interested in convincing myself that nothing extraordinary had happened at all, and there are still days when I would prefer that explanation.

These notes weren't written for an audience.

Most of them started as reminders to myself: where I found something, what caught my attention, why I thought it might matter, and what more ordinary explanation I had considered before deciding to keep it.

For a while that was enough.

Then the collection got larger, and I started noticing relationships between things I had filed months or sometimes years apart. I couldn't keep all of those connections in my head, and eventually the notes became almost as difficult to navigate as the material they were supposed to help me understand.

That's why I built the Index.

Originally it was just an organizational tool.

At least, that's how I understood it at the time.

Most of what is here proves nothing on its own. Some of it is probably irrelevant. Some of it may be wrong, and there are documents I would classify differently now than I did when I first encountered them.

I've left a lot of that intact on purpose.

If I removed every mistake, abandoned interpretation, and dead end, the result would make it look as though the structure had been obvious from the beginning.

It wasn't.

What actually happened was slower and much less satisfying. Individual explanations kept becoming less convincing, while the relationships between different pieces of material became harder for me to dismiss.

I'm aware that those two things can easily be confused.

Losing confidence in one explanation does not automatically make another explanation true.

That distinction is part of the reason these notes still exist.

If you read them looking for proof, I doubt you'll find what you're looking for.

On the other hand, if you come into them expecting a story, there's a good chance you'll create one simply because people are very good at turning scattered events into narratives.

The closest analogy I have is an old road.

One stone doesn't tell you much. A few stones in roughly the same direction are more interesting, but they still might be coincidence. You have to keep following the alignment before you can tell whether you're looking at a road at all.

Even then, knowing that a road exists doesn't tell you who built it or where it goes.

That's more or less where I am.

I don't know where this leads.

These notes are simply the record of how I got this far.
