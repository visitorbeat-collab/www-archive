what_changed

status: provisional classification: reassessment

I've been trying to work out what the resolved reference actually changes.

Not what it suggests.

What it changes.

Those are different questions, and I think I've been letting them blur together.

Before authority_resistance_assessment_001 appeared, I could still explain every problem in the Index using one general assumption:

I am reconstructing incomplete material.

Under that assumption, missing files aren't strange. Neither are broken references, classification errors, contradictions, or relationships that don't make sense yet.

Even the first unexplained cross-reference could still fit inside that model.

A damaged reconstruction is allowed to be messy.

The resolved reference is different for one reason only: I recorded the earlier state before it changed.

That still doesn't tell me what caused the change.

But it does take away an assumption I was relying on more heavily than I realized.

I had been acting as though my own memory of how the Index developed was enough to explain why it looks the way it does now.

Apparently it isn't.

That is the smallest claim I can make, so I'm going to stop there.

I don't have evidence that another intelligence created the file, that the material is changing itself, that anyone is communicating through the Index, or that the encounter is continuing.

What I have is narrower:

I do not currently possess a complete account of how the Index changes.

I don't like that sentence, but I think it's accurate.

Until now, most of my uncertainty concerned where the material came from, what it meant, and how much of my interpretation I could trust.

Now there is another problem: why is a particular document present in the reconstruction at all?

I haven't really had a category for that before.

I probably need one now.

For my own purposes, I'm separating the uncertainty into three kinds.

Source Uncertainty

Can I account for where the material came from?

Interpretation Uncertainty

Can I justify what I think it means?

Reconstruction Uncertainty

Can I account for why it is present in the Index in its current form?

The third category is new.

At least, it is new to my method.

The category is dangerous. "I can't account for this" could easily become "something external did it" whenever I forget an edit or misunderstand ordinary software behavior.

So reconstruction uncertainty needs the same burden as everything else. Before preserving a structural change as unresolved, I need to check editing history, repository state, synchronization, file movement, naming changes, browser behavior, and my own mistakes. If those are enough, then they're enough.

Not external. Unresolved.

This has also made me reconsider my role. Calling myself the author of the Index is convenient, but not accurate. I authored the organization; most of the material was not mine.

I selected it, transcribed it, named it, compared it, linked it, interpreted it, and tried to reconstruct relationships between pieces that were often incomplete.

The Index has always been partly source material and partly my decisions about that material.

Maybe I've been treating authorship as more binary than it actually is.

There are at least three layers here.

There is the source: whatever existed before I reconstructed anything.

There is the reconstruction: everything I deliberately added, translated, classified, linked, or reorganized.

And then there is the structure that emerges from those two interacting.

Until now, I assumed that third layer was completely explainable by the first two.

I'm no longer sure I can make that assumption.

That still doesn't require anything external. Archives, languages, networks, and memory all produce relationships their participants did not consciously design. The Index may simply be too large for me to keep its complete state in my head. That is an ordinary explanation—a good one, actually.

What bothers me is the resemblance to something the recovered material keeps describing.

Again and again, a population discovers that its influence has grown beyond the model it was using to understand that influence.

The response, at least in the material, is not greater certainty.

It's a larger boundary and a smaller claim.

I seem to have reached something similar with the Index.

The resemblance may mean nothing. The methodological response is still useful.

Increase the boundary.
Reduce the claim.
Keep observing.

Before the prospective test, I was asking whether the Index contained evidence of something outside my reconstruction.

I think that question is badly formed now.

It already assumes too much about what the possible answers are.

A better question is:

Which parts of the Index can I account for, and which parts can I not?

That doesn't require me to decide what caused the parts I can't explain.

It just makes the boundary visible.

I can account for most of the Index.

I cannot currently account for all of it.

That's what changed.
