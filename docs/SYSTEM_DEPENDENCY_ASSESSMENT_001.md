# 1.0 Assessment Basis

TP-3 exists within systems whose continued function is required for population persistence.

Relevant supporting systems include:

- atmospheric regulation
- water cycling
- biological productivity
- nutrient cycling
- soil formation
- ecological interaction networks
- climate regulation
- material availability
- energy distribution

TP-3 did not originate these systems.

TP-3 possesses increasing capacity to modify them.

---

# 2.0 Dependency

A population may depend upon a system without understanding that dependency completely.

Observed dependency may be:

**direct**

System failure produces immediate population consequence.

**indirect**

System change alters another system upon which the population depends.

**distributed**

No single component is individually necessary, but collective function is.

**latent**

Dependency becomes visible only following disturbance or loss.

Recognition of dependency varies across TP-3 domains.

---

# 3.0 Influence

TP-3 increasingly modifies dependency-supporting systems through:

- extraction
- construction
- energy use
- biological alteration
- material redistribution
- habitat conversion
- chemical release
- information-driven coordination of population behavior

Many modifications produce substantial local benefit.

Local benefit does not independently indicate system compatibility.

---

# 4.0 Dependency / Influence Overlap

A developmental condition emerges when:

**population depends upon system**

and

**population possesses substantial capacity to alter system**

The population becomes simultaneously:

**beneficiary**

and

**causal participant**

Changes in system condition can no longer be attributed exclusively to external processes.

---

# 5.0 External-System Assumption

TP-3 frequently represents supporting systems as external to population activity.

Observed distinctions include:

- economy / environment
- settlement / habitat
- production / ecology
- infrastructure / landscape
- population activity / planetary condition

These distinctions may be operationally useful.

They are not always causally valid.

Where exchange occurs across the boundary, the systems remain coupled.

---

# 6.0 Boundary Correction

A population cannot accurately evaluate its own stability while excluding systems required for that stability from the assessment boundary.

Where population persistence depends upon biosphere function:

**population stability**

cannot be evaluated independently from:

**biosphere stability**

This does not imply identical interests.

It indicates causal dependency.

---

# 7.0 Replacement Assumption

Technological populations may respond to degraded supporting systems through artificial substitution.

Observed examples include replacement or supplementation of:

- nutrient processes
- water availability
- pollination
- temperature regulation
- biological productivity
- physical habitat
- resource distribution

Substitution may successfully preserve local function.

See:

`environmental_observation_001`

---

# 8.0 Substitution Limits

Artificial replacement of one system function does not necessarily replace all associated functions.

A biological network may contribute simultaneously to:

- productivity
- redundancy
- resilience
- habitat
- nutrient transport
- information transfer
- recovery capacity

Replacement of one visible function may preserve output while reducing total system capacity.

Assessment therefore distinguishes:

**function replaced**

from

**system replaced**

These are not equivalent.

---

# 9.0 Maintenance Relationship

As population influence increases, continued access to system benefits increasingly depends upon population behavior.

The population may therefore enter a maintenance relationship with systems previously experienced as background conditions.

Observed transition:

**system received as condition**

→

**system modified by population**

→

**system persistence partly dependent upon population decisions**

This transition is developmentally significant.

---

# 10.0 Capability Transformation

Before substantial technological influence, system continuity is largely independent of population decision-making.

After substantial technological influence, population decisions may affect:

- system resilience
- recovery capacity
- species persistence
- resource renewal
- atmospheric stability
- long-term habitability

Capability therefore changes the population's relationship to its supporting environment.

The system is no longer merely inhabited.

It is partially governed by population behavior.

---

# 11.0 Non-Ownership

Influence does not establish ownership.

Dependency does not establish ownership.

Technical capacity does not establish ownership.

No evidence supports classification of supporting systems as belonging to the technological population merely because the population can modify them.

Assessment concerns interaction and consequence, not possession.

---

# 12.0 Other Populations

Dependency-supporting systems may also sustain populations without technological representation.

Effects upon these populations remain consequences of TP-3 influence regardless of whether those populations participate in decision-making.

Absence of representation does not eliminate causal inclusion.

Affected-system models that exclude non-participating populations remain incomplete.

---

# 13.0 Temporal Extension

Some supporting-system consequences extend beyond the lifespan of initiating individuals.

Decision impact may therefore affect:

- future members of TP-3
- future non-technological populations
- system states not directly experienced by initiating decision-makers

Direct reciprocity is unavailable across such temporal separation.

Immediate feedback is therefore insufficient as the sole regulatory mechanism.

---

# 14.0 Developmental Relationship

Where a population:

- depends upon a system
- can substantially alter that system
- understands that alteration may affect future system function

the distinction between:

**using the system**

and

**maintaining the system**

becomes increasingly important.

Populations capable only of extraction may persist while unused system capacity remains high.

Persistence becomes less probable as population influence approaches system recovery limits.

---

# 15.0 Assessment Principle

Observed persistent populations tend to incorporate preservation of dependency-supporting conditions into decision models.

This does not require preservation of every existing system state.

Change remains necessary.

Adaptation remains necessary.

Intervention remains necessary.

Relevant distinction:

**change compatible with continued system function**

versus

**change that progressively removes conditions required for persistence**

---

# 16.0 TP-3 Pattern

TP-3 demonstrates increasing recognition of dependency relationships.

Recognition remains uneven across:

- geography
- institutions
- time horizons
- resource systems
- affected populations
- decision frameworks

The population frequently protects supporting systems after dependency becomes directly measurable.

Protection is less consistent where dependency is:

- distributed
- delayed
- probabilistic
- indirect
- difficult to monetize
- difficult to assign to a single decision

---

# 17.0 Assessment Note

TP-3 demonstrates substantial capacity to alter dependency-supporting systems.

Its capacity to represent those systems within decision boundaries is increasing.

Alignment remains incomplete.

Primary developmental variable:

**Does recognition of dependence produce durable modification of influence?**

Continue observation.

# Internal Design Notes

## Purpose

This document introduces stewardship structurally without using the word publicly.

The argument is not:

> Nature is sacred, therefore protect it.

Nor:

> Humans should preserve Earth because it belongs to them.

Instead:

> When an intelligent population becomes capable of altering systems upon which many forms of continued existence depend, maintaining those systems becomes part of managing its own influence.

Stewardship emerges from relationship rather than command.

# Why "Maintenance" Is Used

Maintenance is intentionally colder than stewardship.

The Observers are describing a functional relationship.

The moral or philosophical weight should emerge through interpretation.

A reader should gradually realize:

> If you have enough power to damage the conditions supporting other life and yourself, merely calling those conditions "resources" becomes inadequate.

But the Observers should never preach that sentence.

---

# Non-Ownership Principle

Section 11 is critical.

Stewardship must not imply domination.

The Observer framework rejects:

> We control it, therefore it is ours.

Influence creates consequence.

It does not create entitlement.

This distinction later supports reverence.

---

# Other Populations

Section 12 quietly expands responsibility beyond humanity.

A technological population affects organisms and populations that do not participate in its governance systems.

This begins undermining anthropocentric decision boundaries.

It also moves toward the Observer view of the biosphere as a shared system rather than a human possession.

---

# Temporal Responsibility

Section 13 establishes responsibility toward future conditions without using moral vocabulary.

The problem is structural:

Future populations cannot participate in present decision-making.

Therefore immediate reciprocal feedback cannot regulate all consequential behavior.

This makes long-horizon modeling necessary.

---

# Connection to Stewardship

Internal interpretation:

Stewardship is the behavioral condition in which an intelligent population recognizes that its influence over dependency-supporting systems creates an enduring obligation to maintain their capacity to persist.

Do not expose "obligation" publicly yet.

---

# Connection to Reverence

Internal only.

Maintenance alone is insufficient as the final Observer ideal.

A civilization could maintain ecosystems only because they are useful.

Later material should introduce the possibility that mature intelligence recognizes significance beyond immediate utility.

That is where reverence enters.

For now:

**maintenance before reverence**
