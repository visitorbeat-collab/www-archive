HUMAN TESTIMONY / 002

testimony_002

source: witness recollection verification: unavailable status: retained
The following account is preserved because several aspects correspond with recurring patterns elsewhere in the Index. The encounter cannot be independently verified. The witness consistently distinguishes between direct memory, inference, and later interpretation. Names and identifying details have been removed.

The hardest part to explain is that I don't think they wanted anything from me, at least not in the way people usually mean when they ask that.

I wasn't given a message. Nobody told me to warn anyone or change my life. I don't remember being told to do anything at all.

I'm not even sure they cared that I was frightened.

I remember being in some kind of enclosed place, although I couldn't tell you how large it was. There wasn't an obvious wall directly in front of me, but I had a very strong feeling that I couldn't move past a certain point.

There were figures nearby.

I can't describe them confidently. One seemed closer to me than the others, but even that is the kind of detail I've questioned so many times that I don't know what part belongs to the original memory anymore.

I don't remember speech.

Then something appeared in front of me.

I usually call it a screen when I tell this story because I don't have a better word, but it didn't look like a screen.

It was a landscape.

There were buildings in the distance, a river, farmland, roads. Nothing about it seemed unusual at first.

Then I noticed a structure near the river.

I thought it was industrial. I still think that, although scale was difficult to judge.

And then I had this feeling that I was supposed to make a decision.

I want to separate that from what I actually remember, because nobody told me to choose anything.

I just felt that there was a choice in front of me.

The scene changed after that.

The structure was larger. There were more buildings around it, more roads, more activity. The nearby settlement had grown and the fields looked more organized.

My first reaction was that this was the successful version.

Then somehow time moved forward.

I can't explain how I knew that. Nothing announced it. I just understood that I was seeing the same place later.

The river was lower. Some of the land looked pale or exhausted. There were fewer trees.

But the settlement was still there and the structure was still operating.

Nothing looked like a disaster.

That confused me because I had expected the consequence to be obvious.

Then I saw another version.

The structure stayed smaller. The settlement didn't grow as quickly. There was less activity, and the landscape looked less altered.

I remember assuming this was supposed to be the better choice.

Then that version moved forward too.

There were problems there as well.

Buildings were empty. People seemed to be leaving. There were fewer resources, or at least that's how I interpreted what I saw. I remember something that looked like a shortage of food, but I can't say exactly what I was seeing.

I got frustrated.

What do you want me to choose?

Nothing happened.

I looked toward the figure that seemed closest to me.

I don't remember any reaction.

Then there was another version of the landscape.

And another.

After that it becomes difficult to describe them individually because the point, or what I later decided was the point, was that every decision changed something else.

Sometimes the consequence was immediate. Sometimes it seemed to happen much later.

Sometimes something that looked harmful at first led to a better outcome somewhere else, and sometimes the opposite happened.

I kept trying to work out which version I was supposed to choose.

I couldn't.

That was when the fear changed.

Up until then I had been afraid because I didn't understand where I was or what was happening to me.

Then I became afraid because I realized I had been assuming there had to be a correct answer.

Eventually I stopped trying.

I don't know enough.

The scene stopped after that.

I can't tell you whether it stopped because I thought those words. I don't know if they could hear thoughts, and I don't want to pretend I know.

I'm only describing the order I remember.

Afterward I saw the original landscape again.

Not one of the futures. Just the place as it had first appeared.

This time I noticed things I hadn't paid attention to before.

The river continued beyond the area I had been watching. The road went somewhere else. The fields weren't just empty space between the parts I thought mattered.

There were people downstream.

Animals too. Other settlements.

It became obvious that I had been trying to decide what should happen to one small part of the landscape without understanding what that part was connected to.

That's probably the clearest part of the whole memory.

I remember feeling embarrassed.

Not because I had made the wrong choice.

Because I had been so certain there was one I was supposed to make.

I don't remember anything clearly after that until I was home.

For years I thought the experience was about greed.

Then I decided it was about environmental protection.

Later I thought it was about unintended consequences.

I don't know anymore.

Those may all be interpretations I added afterward because I needed the experience to have a subject.

The part I trust most is much simpler.

I was trying to choose before I understood what the choice affected.

When I finally admitted that I didn't understand the system, everything stopped.

I still don't know what that means.
