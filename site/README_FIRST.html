Before you continue

created: 2027-01-14 revised: 2027-01-14 status: current

If you found this by accident, you're probably looking for something else.

If you came here intentionally, I should explain what you're looking at before you spend too much more time with it.

I usually call this the Index.

I've avoided calling it an archive because I'm not sure that's what it is.

An archive preserves material. An index is supposed to help you find your way through material that already exists.

Most of what is here was not created by me. Some of it I collected. A great deal of it I organized, renamed, compared, or linked. There are also a few things whose presence or history I can't account for as confidently as I would like.

I've tried to keep those categories separate wherever I can.

I'm not asking you to believe any particular explanation for what follows.

There are claims here that I doubt myself, and some that I used to believe more strongly than I do now. Whenever I've found an ordinary explanation that fits the evidence, I've tried to use it.

I've also tried to leave my assumptions visible rather than quietly turning them into facts.

Where practical, I've kept earlier interpretations even after changing my mind. Removing every mistake would make the whole thing look much more coherent than it actually was while I was building it.

If you find an explanation that accounts for the material better than mine does, use it.

This didn't begin with a theory.

It started as a collection of notes I was keeping for myself. The notes became folders, the folders accumulated cross-references, and eventually I found that the relationships between documents were becoming more useful to me than the original subject categories.

That's when I started treating it as an Index rather than just a collection.

There is one distinction I try to maintain throughout the project, although I haven't always done it perfectly.

Observation

Something directly recorded or preserved.

Interpretation

What I think one or more observations may mean.

Speculation

A possibility that I don't currently have enough evidence to support.

Those categories can bleed into each other surprisingly easily, especially after you've been looking at the same material for a long time.

When that happens, the Index becomes less useful.

One reason I've kept so much of this is that I've become increasingly skeptical of memory.

Mine especially, but not only mine.

Memory changes. The way we tell a story changes. Once an interpretation becomes familiar, it can become difficult to remember what the original observation looked like before the interpretation was attached to it.

Writing something down doesn't solve that problem, but at least it leaves something you can return to later.

If you continue, I would suggest reading slowly.

Don't assume that every connection is meaningful just because I've preserved it. But don't assume that calling something a coincidence means the question is settled either.

I don't think certainty or disbelief are especially useful starting positions here.

If a conclusion feels obvious too quickly, it's probably worth checking what assumptions made it feel obvious.

The relationships between documents are often more important than any one document by itself.

Everything beyond this point is arranged according to my best understanding at the time it was added.

Where I don't know something, I've tried to leave the uncertainty visible instead of repairing it into a cleaner story.

If you're not sure where to start, begin with the field notes. They explain how the Index came to exist and, probably more importantly, how my understanding of it changed over time.
