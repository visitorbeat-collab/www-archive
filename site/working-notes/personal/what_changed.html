WORKING NOTE / PERSONAL

what_changed

status: provisional classification: reassessment

I have been trying to decide what the resolved reference actually changes.

Not what it suggests.

What it changes.

Those are different questions.

Before authority_resistance_assessment_001 appeared, I was still able to describe every problem in the Index using the same general model:

I am reconstructing incomplete material.

Under that model:

missing files are expected
broken references are expected
classification errors are expected
contradictions are expected
unexpected relationships are expected

Even the first unexplained cross-reference could remain inside that model.

A damaged reconstruction can produce strange structure.

The resolved reference is different only because I recorded its state beforehand.

That still does not tell me what caused the change.

But it removes one explanation I had relied upon more heavily than I realized:

that my memory of the Index's development was sufficient to account for its current state

It is not.

That is the smallest claim I can make.

So I am keeping it.

I do not have evidence that another intelligence created the file.

I do not have evidence that the source material is changing itself.

I do not have evidence that I am being communicated with.

I do not have evidence that the encounter is continuing.

I do not have evidence that the Index has an external author.

What I have is narrower:

I do not currently possess a complete account of how the Index changes.

That statement is unpleasant because it creates a new category of uncertainty.

Until now I have mostly been uncertain about the material.

Now I am uncertain about the reconstruction process itself.

Those are not the same problem.

source uncertainty

Can I trust the origin of a document?

interpretation uncertainty

Can I trust my reading of it?

reconstruction uncertainty

Can I account for why the document is present in the Index at all?

The third category was not part of my original method.

It is now.

There is an obvious danger here.

Once I permit:

I cannot account for this

it becomes easy to use that phrase everywhere.

A forgotten edit becomes unexplained.

A synchronization problem becomes unexplained.

A bad memory becomes unexplained.

Ordinary software behavior becomes unexplained.

Then "unexplained" quietly becomes "external."

That would destroy the value of the distinction.

So reconstruction uncertainty needs the same burden as everything else.

Before treating a structural event as anomalous, I need to ask whether it can be accounted for by:

editing history
repository state
synchronization
file movement
naming changes
browser behavior
reconstruction error
memory error
ordinary software failure

Only after those remain inadequate should the event be retained as unresolved.

Not external.

Unresolved.

I have also been describing myself as the author of the Index.

That is not quite true.

I am the author of its organization.

I did not author most of what it contains.

I selected.
Transcribed.
Named.
Compared.
Linked.
Interpreted.
Reconstructed.

The Index has always been a relationship between source material and my decisions about that material.

Maybe the mistake was treating authorship as binary.

There are at least three layers:

source

what existed before reconstruction

reconstruction

what I deliberately added, translated, classified, or linked

emergent structure

relationships produced by the interaction of the first two

Until now I assumed the third layer was entirely explainable by the first two.

I no longer know that.

That does not require an external intelligence.

Complex systems produce relationships their participants did not deliberately design.

Archives do this.

Languages do this.

Networks do this.

Memory does this.

Maybe the Index is simply large enough now that I can no longer intuit its complete state.

That is an ordinary explanation.

A good one.

But it creates a strange symmetry with the material itself.

Again and again, the recovered documents describe a population discovering that:

influence exceeds model

and therefore:

certainty must decrease

I appear to have reached the same condition with the Index.

I don't know whether that means anything.

Probably not.

But the methodological response should be the same either way.

Increase the boundary.
Reduce the claim.
Keep observing.

There is one conclusion I am willing to retain.

Before the prospective test, I was trying to determine whether the Index contained evidence of something outside my reconstruction.

Now I think that question is badly formed.

The better question is:

Which parts of the Index can I account for, and which parts can I not?

That does not presume an answer.

I can account for most of it.

I cannot currently account for all of it.

That is what changed.
