Before you continue

created: 2027-01-14 revised: 2027-01-14 status: current

If you found this by accident, you're probably looking for something else.

If you came here intentionally, I should explain what you're looking at before you spend too much more time with it.

I call this the Index. I've avoided calling it an archive because I'm not sure that's what it is. An archive preserves material; an index is supposed to help you find your way through material that already exists.

Most of what is here was not created by me. Some of it I collected. Much of it I organized, renamed, compared, or linked. A few things have a presence or history I cannot account for as confidently as I would like.

This began as notes I was keeping for myself. The notes became folders, the folders accumulated cross-references, and eventually the relationships between documents became more useful than the original subject categories.

I'm not asking you to believe any particular explanation for what follows. Whenever an ordinary explanation fits the evidence, I've tried to use it. I've also kept earlier interpretations where practical, including some I no longer accept. Removing every mistake would make the Index look much more coherent than it was while I was building it.

There is one distinction I try to maintain, although I haven't always done it perfectly.

Observation

Something directly recorded or preserved.

Interpretation

What I think one or more observations may mean.

Speculation

A possibility that I don't currently have enough evidence to support.

Those categories bleed into each other easily, especially after the same material has been examined for a long time. Memory changes too. Once an interpretation becomes familiar, it can become difficult to remember what the original observation looked like before the interpretation was attached to it.

Writing something down does not solve that problem, but it leaves something that can be checked later.

If you continue, read slowly. Do not assume every preserved connection is meaningful, but do not assume that calling something a coincidence settles the question either. If an explanation accounts for the material better than mine does, use it.

If you're not sure where to start, begin with the field notes. They record how the Index came to exist and how my understanding of it changed.
