HUMAN TESTIMONY / 002

testimony_002

source: witness recollection verification: unavailable status: retained
The following account is preserved because several aspects correspond with recurring patterns elsewhere in the Index. The encounter cannot be independently verified. The witness consistently distinguishes between direct memory, inference, and later interpretation. Names and identifying details have been removed.

The hardest part to explain is that I don't think they wanted anything from me.

Not in the way people usually mean that.

I wasn't given a message.

I wasn't told to warn anyone.

I wasn't told to change my life.

I wasn't even sure whether they knew I was frightened.

I remember being somewhere enclosed.

I could not tell how large the space was.

There was no obvious wall in front of me, but I had a strong sense that I could not move beyond a certain point.

There were figures nearby.

I could not see them clearly enough to describe them with confidence.

I remember one of them being closer than the others.

I do not remember speech.

Then something appeared in front of me.

I don't know whether it was a screen.

It did not look like a screen.

I saw a landscape.

There were buildings in the distance.

A river.

Agricultural land.

Roads.

Nothing unusual.

Then I became aware of a structure near the river.

Industrial, I think.

It was difficult to understand scale.

I had the feeling that I was supposed to do something.

That part is important.

Nobody told me that.

I simply felt that there was a decision.

Then the scene changed.

I saw what I believed was the result of one possible choice.

The structure expanded.

There were more buildings.

More roads.

The nearby settlement became larger.

The fields looked more organized.

There was activity everywhere.

It looked successful.

Then the image moved forward.

I don't know how I knew time had passed.

The river was lower.

Some of the land looked pale.

There were fewer trees.

The settlement was still there.

The structure was still operating.

Nothing looked catastrophic.

That confused me.

Then I saw another version.

The structure remained smaller.

The settlement grew more slowly.

There was less activity.

The river looked different.

The landscape looked less altered.

I remember thinking that this was supposed to be the "good" choice.

Then that version moved forward too.

There were problems there as well.

Fewer resources.

People leaving.

Buildings abandoned.

Something that looked like food shortages.

I became frustrated.

What do you want me to choose?

Nothing happened.

I looked toward the figure closest to me.

No reaction.

Then I saw another version.

And another.

Every choice changed something else.

Sometimes the results were obvious.

Sometimes they were delayed.

Sometimes a decision that looked bad at first produced something better later.

Sometimes the opposite happened.

I started trying to find the correct answer.

I could not.

That was the point where I became afraid in a different way.

I realized I had been assuming there was one.

I stopped trying to choose.

I don't know enough.

The scene stopped.

I cannot say whether that was because of what I thought.

I do not know whether they could hear thoughts.

I am only describing the sequence as I remember it.

After that I was shown the landscape again.

Not one future.

Just the original place.

I noticed things I had ignored before.

The river connected to other water.

The road continued beyond the area I had been watching.

The fields were not empty land.

There were people downstream.

There were animals.

There were other settlements.

I had been trying to decide what should happen to one place without knowing what that place was connected to.

That is the clearest memory I have.

I remember feeling embarrassed.

Not because I chose badly.

Because I had been so eager to choose.

I don't remember anything after that until I was home.

For years I interpreted the experience as a lesson about greed.

Then I thought it was about environmental protection.

Later I thought it was about consequences.

Now I am not sure it was any of those things.

The part that stays with me is the moment I stopped looking for the correct choice and admitted that I did not understand the system.

That felt more important than any of the choices.
