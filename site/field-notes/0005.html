The Road

created: various revised: various status: retained

I've spent years trying to find a useful way to explain this project to myself.

The comparison I keep coming back to is archaeology.

Imagine finding several disconnected pieces of an old road. None of them tells you very much by itself, and at first there may be no reason to assume they belong to the same route.

But if enough of them line up, you start estimating where the missing sections ought to be.

That doesn't mean you're inventing the road. At least, not necessarily.

You're trying to reconstruct something from what survived.

For a long time, that was exactly how I thought about the Index.

Each new document was another surviving section. My job was to work out how the pieces related without filling the gaps more aggressively than the evidence allowed.

What I didn't think about at first was the obvious part of the analogy.

Roads usually go somewhere.

I don't remember exactly when that started bothering me. It wasn't a sudden realization so much as a question that became harder to avoid as the Index grew.

If I am reconstructing a path rather than merely arranging fragments, then at what point do I have to ask whether the direction itself means something?

And there is another problem that I still don't know how to solve.

How do you tell the difference between a map you've built from your own expectations and a landscape that was already there before you started drawing?

I used to think careful documentation would eventually make that distinction obvious.

I'm less confident about that now.

So I keep following the fragments and trying not to decide in advance what they're supposed to become.

Maybe there is a road.

Maybe I've become very good at arranging stones.

For now, I don't think I can honestly rule out either possibility.
