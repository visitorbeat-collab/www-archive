1.0Assessment Basis

TP-3 demonstrates increasing capacity to alter systems beyond the scale of direct individual perception.

Relevant capabilities include modification of:

material distribution
ecological networks
atmospheric composition
energy transfer
biological populations
information environments
population behavior
long-duration resource availability

Intervention capacity continues to increase.

2.0Capability Growth

Technological capability allows TP-3 to produce effects across progressively larger boundaries.

individual action
↓
local system modification
↓
regional system modification
↓
network modification
↓
biosphere-scale influence

Magnitude of possible intervention has increased substantially across the observation interval.

Rate of increase remains high.

3.0Prediction Capacity

Prediction capacity has also increased.

TP-3 demonstrates significant ability to model:

immediate physical effects
short-term system response
resource requirements
probable local outcomes
common failure conditions

Prediction remains less reliable where effects involve:

long temporal intervals
multiple interacting systems
nonlinear feedback
emergent behavior
low-frequency events
irreversible transition

4.0Divergence

Capability and prediction do not increase at equal rates.

In multiple observed domains:

ability to produce change
exceeds
ability to predict full consequence

The difference between these capacities is classified:

intervention uncertainty

Intervention uncertainty increases developmental risk when modification is difficult to reverse.

5.0Reversibility

Consequences vary in reversibility.

high reversibility

Original conditions can be restored with limited additional influence.

partial reversibility

Restoration is possible but incomplete or resource-intensive.

low reversibility

Previous system state cannot reliably be restored.

unknown reversibility

System behavior following intervention is insufficiently understood.

Increasing capability has expanded the number of systems TP-3 can alter before reversibility is established.

6.0Decision Threshold

TP-3 commonly evaluates intervention according to expected benefit.

Additional variables are included inconsistently:

uncertainty
system boundary
delayed consequence
recovery capacity
reversibility
affected populations outside the initiating group

The probability that an intervention proceeds does not decline consistently as uncertainty increases.

7.0Capability Asymmetry

The following condition has become increasingly frequent:

technical capacity sufficient for intervention
predictive capacity insufficient for complete consequence estimation

This condition does not independently indicate developmental instability.

No advanced population possesses complete predictive capacity.

The relevant variable is behavioral response to the asymmetry.

8.0Response Categories

proceed

Intervention occurs despite unresolved consequence.

reduce

Intervention magnitude is limited.

delay

Intervention is postponed pending additional information.

test

Intervention occurs at reduced scale to improve prediction.

abandon

Intervention is not pursued because uncertainty exceeds accepted threshold.

TP-3 demonstrates all five responses.

Frequency varies by domain.

9.0Restraint Capacity

Ability to perform an intervention does not reliably predict whether intervention will occur.

TP-3 demonstrates capacity for deliberate non-use of available capability.

This is significant.

Restraint is more frequently observed where:

consequence is immediate
affected populations are visible
failure is reversible only at high cost
institutional accountability is strong
uncertainty is explicitly represented in decision models

Restraint decreases where consequence is delayed, distributed, or difficult to attribute.

10.0Influence Expansion

As capability increases, the number of systems affected by a single decision may exceed the number of systems represented within the decision model.

decision boundary < consequence boundary

Where this occurs, successful local outcomes may coexist with unmodeled external effects.

See: environmental_observation_001

See: environmental_observation_002

11.0Developmental Significance

Capability is not sufficient evidence of developmental maturity.

High capability increases the range of possible outcomes.

It therefore increases the consequence of:

inaccurate models
delayed revision
narrow system boundaries
short prediction horizons
failure to represent uncertainty

The developmental significance of error increases with the magnitude of available influence.

12.0Comparative Principle

Two populations may possess equivalent predictive accuracy.

If one population possesses substantially greater intervention capability, identical predictive error does not produce equivalent consequence.

Assessment must therefore include both:

probability of error
magnitude of effect if error occurs

Capability changes the significance of uncertainty.

13.0Assessment Note

TP-3 continues to increase intervention capacity.

Predictive capacity also increases but remains incomplete.

This condition is expected.

Primary assessment concern is not incomplete prediction.

Primary assessment concern is whether increasing uncertainty and increasing consequence produce corresponding changes in:

decision threshold
intervention scale
reversibility requirements
testing behavior
willingness to delay
willingness not to act

Continue observation.
