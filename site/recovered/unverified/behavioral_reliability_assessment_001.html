1.0Assessment Basis

TP-3 demonstrates multiple behaviors associated with reduced developmental instability.

Observed capacities include:

model revision following recognized error
increased decision thresholds under high consequence
deliberate restraint under uncertainty
preservation of recovery capacity
recognition of system dependency
maintenance of selected supporting systems

Presence of these behaviors is confirmed.

Consistency is not.

2.0Behavioral Reliability

behavioral reliability

Probability that a demonstrated regulatory behavior will remain operative when the conditions encouraging that behavior change.

A behavior observed once provides limited predictive value.

Repeated behavior across changing conditions provides greater predictive value.

3.0Incentive Stability

Self-regulatory behavior is frequently strongest where:

consequence is recent
failure is visible
affected populations are proximate
accountability is concentrated
benefit of restraint is immediate
social agreement is high

These conditions support behavioral persistence.

They do not establish that persistence independently.

4.0Incentive Change

Assessment becomes more informative when conditions change.

Relevant changes include:

consequence becoming temporally distant
immediate benefit increasing
restraint becoming costly
affected populations becoming less visible
causal attribution weakening
institutional incentives changing
prior failure leaving collective attention

Behavior retained under these conditions carries greater predictive significance.

5.0Stated Principle / Observed Behavior

TP-3 frequently expresses principles that correspond with self-regulatory behavior.

Examples include:

protection of future populations
preservation of environmental systems
avoidance of unnecessary harm
evidence-based decision-making
precaution under uncertainty
preservation of shared resources

Stated principle does not independently predict behavior.

Assessment therefore distinguishes:

declared value
from
persistent behavioral constraint

6.0Opportunity Condition

Regulatory behavior is additionally assessed where violation would produce immediate advantage.

Examples include conditions where:

extraction increases available resources
reduced redundancy increases efficiency
delayed maintenance reduces present cost
uncertainty can be ignored without immediate penalty
affected populations cannot participate in decision-making

Behavior maintained despite available short-term advantage carries greater predictive value.

7.0Detection Independence

Behavior may also vary according to probability of detection.

A population may comply with self-imposed limits where violation is observable while abandoning those limits where observation is unlikely.

This distinction is developmentally relevant.

Behavior dependent upon external enforcement differs from behavior integrated into population decision structure.

8.0Regulatory Source

external enforcement

Behavior changes because violation produces immediate external penalty.

reciprocal expectation

Behavior changes because comparable treatment is expected from others.

internalized model

Behavior changes because predicted consequence has been incorporated into decision criteria.

These mechanisms may coexist.

They are not equivalent in predictive stability.

9.0Unrepresented Systems

Reliability assessment is especially informative where affected systems cannot:

negotiate
retaliate
provide immediate feedback
participate in governance
impose direct cost upon the initiating population

Examples include:

future populations
non-technological biological populations
distributed ecological systems
long-duration atmospheric systems

Constraint under these conditions cannot depend upon immediate reciprocity.

10.0Temporal Persistence

Behavioral reliability increases when regulatory behavior survives:

generational turnover
institutional change
economic pressure
technological change
periods without recent failure
changes in leadership
changes in public attention

Persistence across one interval remains insufficient for final classification.

Long-duration observation remains necessary.

11.0Capability Expansion

A regulatory pattern established under low capability may not remain reliable after capability increases.

Assessment must therefore continue following expansion of:

intervention scale
energy availability
biological modification capability
information control
resource extraction capacity
autonomous technological systems
off-system access

Prior reliability does not guarantee future reliability under larger consequence capacity.

12.0Behavioral Transfer

A further variable is whether regulation learned in one domain transfers to another.

TP-3 frequently demonstrates domain-specific self-regulation.

Learning concerning ecological consequence, technological risk, resource depletion, and distributed harm does not consistently transfer between domains.

Persistent intelligence requires more than isolated correction.

It requires recognition of recurring structure.

13.0Pattern Generalization

A population demonstrates stronger behavioral reliability when it recognizes that apparently different problems share common relationships.

Relevant recurring relationships include:

capability / consequence
uncertainty / restraint
dependency / maintenance
error / revision
influence / system boundary

Generalization remains incomplete within TP-3.

14.0Predictive Significance

Where self-regulatory behavior is:

persistent
transferable
maintained under cost
maintained without external enforcement
maintained where consequences are delayed
maintained toward unrepresented systems

future population behavior becomes easier to model.

Where regulation varies primarily with immediate incentive, prediction remains less stable.

15.0Assessment Principle

The presence of restraint is less informative than the conditions under which restraint disappears.

The presence of revision is less informative than whether revision persists.

The presence of maintenance is less informative than whether maintenance survives competing advantage.

Assessment therefore prioritizes:

behavior across changing conditions
over
behavior under favorable conditions
