HUMAN TESTIMONY / 001

testimony_001

The following account was preserved because several elements correspond with material elsewhere in the Index. No independent verification of the described encounter exists. The witness's interpretation should not be treated as equivalent to the event itself. Names and identifying details have been removed.

I don't know exactly when it started, and that's still one of the things that bothers me.

I remember being awake in bed. I remember getting up. After that, it stops feeling like a normal sequence of memories and turns into pieces.

The next thing I can describe clearly is being somewhere very bright.

I don't remember entering a room, if it was a room. The light didn't seem to come from a lamp or a ceiling or anywhere I could point to. It was just there.

There were figures around me. I wish I could describe them better, but whenever I try, I feel like I'm filling in details that may not actually belong to the memory.

I don't remember their faces clearly.

I was frightened. Very frightened, actually, but they didn't seem interested in that.

They weren't threatening me, at least not in any way I remember. They weren't trying to reassure me either. They seemed occupied with whatever they were doing.

The closest comparison I have is being examined.

That may be the wrong word, but it's the one that keeps coming back.

Then something changed.

I don't know whether the room disappeared or whether I stopped being aware of it.

Suddenly I had this overwhelming sense of being very high above the ground.

I could see the Earth.

I know how that sounds.

I remember the curve of it, cloud systems, the atmosphere, large areas in darkness. It was beautiful at first, which is something I don't usually mention because what came after has mostly replaced that part in my memory.

Then the view changed.

I saw forests disappearing.

Not one place. It seemed like many places, one after another.

I saw water changing color. I remember dead fish, although I couldn't tell you where they were or even whether I was seeing one location continuously.

There were areas of ground that looked dry and damaged. Fires. Then cities, enormous ones, roads and lights and industrial areas.

Everything was happening too quickly for me to understand it properly.

What frightened me was the sense that the images belonged together.

I had this immediate feeling that I was being shown what we were doing.

They want us to stop.

But I need to be very clear about that sentence.

I don't remember anyone saying it.

There was no voice telling me we were destroying the planet. No sentence, no instruction, nothing like that.

That's what I understood from what I was seeing.

I don't know whether that distinction mattered to me at the time. It matters to me now.

After that, the images became harder to describe.

I remember something that looked like a network, except "network" makes it sound mechanical and it wasn't. It was more like veins or roots, maybe both.

Forests, water, animals, cities, people — all of it seemed connected.

If something changed in one place, something else changed somewhere far away.

I couldn't follow all of it. I remember trying to and getting overwhelmed because there was too much happening at once.

I wanted it to stop.

Then I saw people again.

Crowds, traffic, buildings, stores, screens. People buying things. Throwing things away. Going about normal life.

Nothing dramatic was happening.

For some reason that was worse than the fires.

Nobody seemed to know that anything was wrong.

I remember thinking that we were acting as though the planet was somewhere beneath us instead of something we were part of.

That's probably not exactly how I thought it at the time. That's how I would say it now.

But the feeling stayed.

I don't remember coming back.

The next clear memory is sitting on the edge of my bed in the dark.

For several days afterward I was completely certain I had been shown a warning.

I'm not that certain anymore.

I know memory changes. I know dreams can feel completely real, and I know that once you decide what an experience meant, that interpretation can start changing the memory itself.

So I can't say with confidence that they showed me we were destroying the planet.

I can say that, at the time, that is what I believed I had been shown.

Those may not be the same thing.
