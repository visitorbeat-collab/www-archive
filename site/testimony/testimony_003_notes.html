The phrase:

THE ANSWER CHANGES THE TEST

is exactly the kind of thing I need to be careful with.

Not because I think it's obviously false.

Because it's almost too useful.

A short sentence that seems to explain several different parts of the Index at once is very easy to give too much weight.

I've caught myself doing that before.

Something fits neatly, the relationship suddenly feels obvious, and the feeling of resolution starts doing work that the evidence hasn't actually done.

I don't want that to happen here.

The phrase does seem compatible with several ideas already present in the Index: cognitive assessment, withheld explanation, developmental autonomy, source-authority effects, and the possibility that giving a subject an answer changes whatever is being measured.

That compatibility is real.

I just don't know how much it means.

There are several questions bundled together here, and I need to keep them separate.

Did the witness actually write the phrase when they say they did?

If so, did it originate during the experience, or was it something produced afterward?

Does the sentence accurately describe what happened to the witness?

And even if it does, is that structure actually related to the recovered material?

A yes to one of those does not give me a yes to the others.

The third question is probably the one that worries me most.

The phrase is extremely efficient.

It takes a complicated and uncertain experience and compresses it into a principle that feels immediately understandable.

That may be useful.

It may also hide how much uncertainty was present before the sentence existed.

I've seen this happen elsewhere in the Index.

A useful sentence becomes a label. The label becomes a category. After enough material gets filed under that category, the category starts looking like the explanation for the material that created it in the first place.

Once that happens, questioning the original idea gets much harder because the whole structure has started depending on it.

I don't want to build that kind of loop around this phrase.

So I'm keeping it exactly where it came from.

It remains witness material.

I'm not treating it as recovered terminology, and I'm not using it as a key for interpreting everything around it.

At least not yet.

There is also a simpler problem.

I want it to be important.

I noticed that almost immediately.

That doesn't make the phrase meaningless, but it does mean I should demand more from it than I would if I didn't care what it turned out to be.
