1.0Assessment Basis

Transition is not developmentally neutral.

Any process that increases a technological population's access to:

information
energy
mobility
coordination
intervention capacity
external systems
other technological populations

may alter the scale of consequence available to that population.

Assessment must therefore evaluate not only:

current population condition

but also:

population condition after transition

2.0Transition

transition

A change in population access that materially expands one or more forms of influence.

Transition may include:

information transfer
technological transfer
environmental access
system access
mobility expansion
coordination expansion
participation in wider relational networks

Specific transition class unresolved.

3.0Capability Delta

Relevant variable:

capability delta

The difference between:

pre-transition influence capacity

and:

post-transition influence capacity

Small capability delta may produce limited developmental disturbance.

Large capability delta may invalidate prior behavioral assessment.

4.0Assessment Instability

A population may appear reliably regulated within a known capability range.

Where transition significantly increases capability:

prior behavioral reliability cannot be assumed to remain valid.

Relevant question becomes:

Does regulatory capacity scale with newly available influence?

5.0Consequence Expansion

Transition may increase consequence through:

greater intervention range
reduced action cost
increased speed
increased reversibility asymmetry
access to previously isolated systems
expanded affected populations
shorter delay between intention and effect
larger coordination scale

Even unchanged population behavior may therefore produce greater consequence after transition.

6.0Reliability Transfer

Behavioral reliability is not automatically transferable across capability states.

Observed regulation under:

capability state A

does not establish equivalent regulation under:

capability state B

where B permits substantially greater consequence.

Assessment must account for capability discontinuity.

7.0Transition Risk

Transition risk increases where:

capability delta is large
behavioral reliability is unresolved
regulatory integration is incomplete
consequence scale is difficult to reverse
affected systems are poorly modeled
cross-domain generalization is weak
external enforcement would be required after transfer

No single variable independently determines outcome.

8.0Trust Requirement

Where transition risk increases:

required predictive confidence should increase.

Therefore:

greater possible transition consequence
requires
greater confidence in post-transition regulation

This condition resembles decision-threshold proportionality at population scale.

9.0Maturity / Trust Distinction

Developmental maturity and transition trustworthiness are related but not equivalent.

Maturity concerns:

integration of regulatory capacities within the population

Trustworthiness concerns:

predictive confidence that those capacities will remain operative after conditions change

Transition changes conditions.

Therefore maturity alone is insufficient.

10.0Stable Population / Unstable Transition

A population may be developmentally stable under current capability while transition remains high risk.

Example condition:

current restraint sufficient
current consequence bounded
current systems familiar
current institutions adapted

but:

transition introduces unfamiliar capability
affected system boundary expands rapidly
consequence exceeds existing governance capacity
feedback becomes delayed or weak

In such cases:

population stability
does not imply
transition stability

11.0Capability Shock

Where capability expansion occurs faster than regulatory adaptation:

capability shock

Possible effects include:

governance delay
norm instability
incentive exploitation
rapid diffusion before regulation
cross-domain application without transfer of restraint
irreversible consequence before model revision

Capability shock increases transition risk even in populations demonstrating substantial maturity.

12.0Gradual Transition

Transition risk may be reduced where access expands incrementally.

Possible advantages include:

behavioral observation between stages
regulatory adaptation
model revision
detection of cross-domain failure
improved consequence prediction
lower reversibility cost
controlled capability diffusion

Incremental transition permits reassessment.

13.0Withholding

Delay of transition does not necessarily indicate rejection.

Where reliability remains unresolved:

withholding additional capability may reduce total developmental risk.

Relevant distinction:

withholding due to punishment
versus
withholding due to consequence uncertainty

Only the second is supported by present model.

14.0Non-Intervention

Non-transition also carries consequence.

Delayed access may affect:

population development
system stability
internal conflict
technological trajectory
ecological recovery
future contact probability

Therefore transition assessment must compare:

risk of transition
with
risk of continued non-transition

Neither condition is consequence-free.

15.0Timing

Optimal transition point cannot be defined solely by maximum maturity.

Delay may allow:

stronger regulation
improved prediction
greater persistence
better cross-domain generalization

but may also allow:

greater independent capability growth
accumulated environmental damage
conflict escalation
irreversible system degradation

Transition timing therefore requires competing-risk comparison.

16.0Minimum Condition

Transition should not proceed where:

expected increase in consequence
substantially exceeds
expected increase in regulatory capacity

unless delayed transition produces greater total risk.

This is not a fixed threshold.

It is a proportional assessment.

17.0External Population Risk

Transition also creates risk to systems beyond TP-3.

Possible affected categories include:

other technological populations
non-technological biospheres
shared environments
information systems
transport systems
inter-population networks

Assessment therefore cannot remain bounded to TP-3 outcomes alone.

18.0Trust as Consequence-Weighted Confidence

For transition purposes, predictive confidence should be weighted by possible consequence.

Equivalent uncertainty produces different risk under different capability deltas.

Therefore:

trust requirement is consequence-dependent

A population need not become perfectly predictable.

Required confidence changes with what is being transferred.

19.0TP-3 Assessment

Developmental maturity: indeterminate
Behavioral reliability: unresolved
Regulatory integration: partial
Cross-domain generalization: incomplete
Capability growth: high
Transition capability delta: unresolved
Post-transition consequence range: unresolved

Transition risk therefore cannot be classified as acceptable.

20.0Assessment Principle

Transition is not a reward granted after maturity.

It is an intervention whose consequences depend upon whether regulation survives the capability it introduces.

21.0Status

Transition risk: unresolved
Predictive confidence: insufficient
Capability delta: unclassified
Non-transition risk: unresolved
Transition recommendation: defer

Continue observation.

No capability transfer recommended.
