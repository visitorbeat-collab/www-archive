2.0Two Questions

Is the material coherent?

Can its presence be accounted for?

A positive answer to the first does not answer the second. Failure to answer the second does not establish external authorship.

3.0Self-Application

The document defines authority resistance as continued independent judgment under capability asymmetry. Accepting it because its arrival feels unusual would reproduce the failure it describes:

unexplained provenance → special status → reduced scrutiny

An anomaly should receive more scrutiny, not less.

Unexplained events can accumulate authority through rarity, apparent intention, or the feeling that they arrived at the right moment. None of those qualities demonstrates that a source is capable, accurate, or even external. Uncertainty can raise perceived significance without raising reliability.

4.0Coherence and Contamination

The file fits source authority, epistemic dependency, developmental autonomy, reciprocal trust, and Testimony 003. That may indicate common conceptual origin. It may also indicate expected reconstruction: by this stage, I already had enough material to anticipate such a file. Compatibility has reduced discriminatory value when the expected concept is already known.

Contamination can therefore run in both directions. Earlier testimony may be read through recovered language, while later “recovered” material may be read through concepts I have already built. A perfect fit is not always stronger evidence. Sometimes it is exactly what reconstruction predicts.

The recursion is notable: a document about resisting source authority has become a source-authority problem. That makes the event narratively precise. It does not make intentional design the only cause.
