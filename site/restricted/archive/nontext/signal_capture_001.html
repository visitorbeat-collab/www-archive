OBSERVATION

Voyager 1's Plasma Wave Instrument detected oscillations of electrons in ionized gas beyond the heliosphere. The two visible groups were recorded during October–November 2012 and April–May 2013.

Frequency provides a measure of local electron density. The later activity occurs at a higher frequency, indicating that the spacecraft was moving through denser interstellar plasma.

REPRESENTATION

The instrument did not record pressure waves moving through an atmosphere. It measured plasma oscillations through its antennas. Because those oscillations fall within the range of human hearing, their amplitude and frequency can be amplified and reproduced through a speaker.

The visible plot and audible source record are two human representations of the same measured relationship. Neither should be mistaken for unmediated perception of interstellar space.

INTERPRETATION CONSTRAINT

Structured variation does not establish communication. In this record, repeated rising tones have a physical explanation connected to changing plasma density and solar activity.

The record demonstrates why pattern must be evaluated with instrument context. The same visible structure, detached from its acquisition history, could invite a substantially different interpretation.
