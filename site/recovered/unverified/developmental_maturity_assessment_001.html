1.0Assessment Basis

Technological capability does not independently indicate developmental maturity.

A population may demonstrate:

advanced prediction
large-scale coordination
high energy capture
biological intervention
rapid information exchange
environmental modification
complex social organization

while remaining developmentally unstable.

Assessment therefore distinguishes:

capability
from
maturity

2.0Maturity

Maturity does not refer to age, duration, technological level, or accumulated knowledge.

Maturity refers to the degree to which increasing influence remains integrated with capacities that regulate consequence.

Relevant capacities include:

interpretation
model revision
restraint
uncertainty recognition
system-boundary expansion
long-horizon reasoning
maintenance of dependency-supporting systems
preservation under uncertain utility
behavioral persistence

No single capacity is sufficient.

3.0Integration

A population may demonstrate individual regulatory capacities without integrating them.

Examples:

recognizing uncertainty without altering action
revising models without preserving revision
preserving ecosystems only where direct utility is known
exercising restraint in one domain while expanding risk in another
recognizing consequence after failure but not before intervention

Assessment therefore prioritizes:

relationship between capacities
over
presence of capacities in isolation

4.0Capability / Regulation Relationship

As capability increases:

affected system scale may increase
consequence may become more distributed
reversibility may decrease
causal pathways may become more complex
delayed effects may increase
affected populations may extend beyond decision-makers

Regulatory capacity must therefore change with capability.

Static restraint applied to expanding influence becomes proportionally weaker.

5.0Proportionality

Developmental maturity requires increasing correspondence between:

capacity to produce consequence
and
capacity to regulate consequence

Where capability increases faster than regulation:

developmental instability increases

Where regulation increases proportionally:

developmental stability increases

6.0Revision

Error occurrence does not independently indicate immaturity.

All predictive systems produce error.

Greater developmental information is available from:

recognition of error
correction of model
transfer of correction across domains
persistence of correction after immediate consequence disappears

Repeated error without model change provides stronger negative information than error itself.

7.0Restraint

A mature population does not require complete prediction before action.

Complete prediction is unavailable.

Relevant behavior is proportional restraint.

Where:

uncertainty increases
consequence increases
reversibility decreases
affected boundary expands

decision conditions should change.

Restraint is therefore not equivalent to inactivity.

It is regulation of intervention scale under incomplete understanding.

8.0Stewardship

Where population stability depends upon systems substantially modified by population behavior, maintenance becomes developmentally relevant.

Maturity requires increasing integration of:

dependency recognition
system maintenance
long-horizon preservation
recovery capacity
effects upon non-participating populations

A population unable to maintain systems upon which its own persistence depends remains developmentally unstable regardless of technical capability.

9.0Preservation Beyond Dependency

System maintenance based solely upon direct utility remains incomplete.

Where influence extends to systems whose utility is:

uncertain
indirect
absent
unknown
not population-centered

mature decision models may still assign preservation significance.

This is not required because all existing states must persist.

It is required because:

absence of demonstrated utility
does not establish
absence of significance

10.0Non-Ownership

Increasing ability to modify a system does not establish ownership of that system.

Mature populations increasingly distinguish:

control
from
entitlement

and:

influence
from
exclusive significance

This distinction becomes more important as technical capability expands.

11.0Cross-Domain Generalization

Developmental maturity requires transfer of regulatory principles between domains.

A population that recognizes irreversible ecological loss but ignores irreversible technological risk has not fully generalized the relationship.

A population that increases evidentiary standards for medical intervention but not atmospheric intervention demonstrates domain-specific regulation.

A population that preserves redundancy in infrastructure but eliminates redundancy in biological systems may recognize function without recognizing structure.

Generalization remains a major TP-3 uncertainty.

12.0Internal / External Regulation

Regulation may be imposed externally or maintained internally.

External regulation may reduce harmful behavior.

It does not independently establish maturity.

Assessment therefore distinguishes:

behavior prevented by external consequence
from
behavior constrained by integrated model

Stable developmental regulation increasingly persists where immediate external enforcement is absent.

13.0Immediate Benefit

Maturity is most informative when regulatory behavior conflicts with immediate advantage.

Relevant conditions include:

profitable extraction
accelerated development
reduced redundancy
delayed maintenance
irreversible intervention
competitive advantage
low probability of immediate penalty

Behavior under favorable conditions provides limited information.

Behavior under competing incentive provides greater information.

14.0Temporal Scale

Developmental maturity cannot be classified from short-duration performance.

Relevant behaviors must persist across:

consequence cycles
leadership change
economic change
technological change
generational turnover
declining memory of prior failure

Short-term coordination may indicate adaptive capacity.

Long-term persistence indicates deeper integration.

15.0Population Scale

Mature behavior need not occur uniformly among all individuals.

Assessment concerns population-level structure.

Relevant questions include:

Do institutions preserve revision?
Do successful constraints survive leadership change?
Does knowledge alter future decision thresholds?
Can local learning transfer into broader systems?
Can minority recognition become durable population behavior?

Individual insight is developmentally relevant only where it can influence persistent system behavior.

16.0Contradiction

TP-3 demonstrates contradictory developmental indicators.

Examples include:

advanced prediction with short decision horizons
sophisticated ecological modeling with continued ecological degradation
formal uncertainty analysis with high-consequence intervention
strong preservation behavior alongside continued irreversible loss
rapid correction in some domains and persistent failure in others

Contradiction does not invalidate assessment.

Contradiction is itself developmental information.

17.0Asynchrony

Technological populations do not mature uniformly.

Different capacities may develop at different rates.

Possible sequence:

capability expansion
before
consequence recognition
before
regulatory adaptation
before
persistent integration

Developmental instability is greatest where delay between these stages becomes large.

18.0Maturity Condition

No single action establishes maturity.

No single crisis establishes maturity.

No single institution establishes maturity.

No single generation establishes maturity.

Maturity is increasingly supported where:

capability and restraint remain proportional
error produces durable revision
uncertainty alters action
system boundaries expand with consequence
dependency produces maintenance
significance is recognized beyond direct utility
regulatory behavior survives changing incentive

20.0Assessment Principle

Maturity is not demonstrated by what a population can do.

It is demonstrated by whether the conditions governing what it chooses to do become more demanding as its influence increases.
