0006 / FIELD NOTE

First Inconsistency

created: various revised: various status: retained

I think people tend to imagine that important discoveries arrive looking important.

That has not been my experience.

Most of the things that have changed the way I think about this project started out as annoyances.

This one was a broken cross-reference.

I noticed it while going through some older notes. The reference pointed to a file I couldn't find anywhere in the project.

My first assumption was that I had renamed it and forgotten. That happens.

When that turned up nothing, I assumed I had deleted it during some earlier cleanup and left the reference behind.

I searched the project, then my backups, then a few older exports.

Nothing.

At that point I treated it like any other dead reference and removed it.

I didn't think much more about it.

About a week later I noticed the same reference again.

At first I assumed I had somehow restored an older version of the file, but the wording wasn't identical to what I remembered deleting. It had been rewritten.

That was when I checked the revision history.

The deletion was there.

I could not find a corresponding edit that explained the reference returning.

That doesn't mean there wasn't one. Revision history is not magic, and neither is my memory. There are plenty of ordinary ways I could have lost track of a change.

But it was the first time I had encountered something in the structure of the Index that I couldn't immediately connect to an action I remembered taking.

That bothered me more than the missing file itself.

Up to that point, I had been comfortable separating two kinds of uncertainty.

I often didn't know what the material meant.

I sometimes didn't know why two documents seemed related.

But I assumed I knew why the Index looked the way it did, because I was the one arranging it.

After this, I stopped being quite so confident about that.

I didn't conclude that anyone else had changed it.

I still don't think I can say that.

What changed was simpler: from that point on, I started treating unexplained changes in the structure differently from unexplained meanings inside it.

Looking back, that was probably the first real change in the project.

Not because I had found proof of anything unusual.

Because I had found something I could no longer explain entirely by remembering what I had done.
