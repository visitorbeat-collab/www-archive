The Pattern Problem

created: various revised: various status: retained

One of the things I had to get used to early on was how unreliable pattern recognition can be.

We depend on it for almost everything, which is part of the problem. The same ability that lets us notice a real relationship also makes it very easy to connect things that have nothing to do with each other.

For a long time I assumed that was what was happening to me.

Once you spend enough time with the same material, coincidence starts to feel less like coincidence. You learn the vocabulary, you notice recurring shapes, and after a while almost anything can be made to fit if you want it badly enough.

That worried me enough that I started keeping a fairly simple rule.

Record what happened before deciding what it means.

I haven't always been good at following it, but it helps.

If an interpretation keeps surviving new material, contradiction, and time, then maybe it deserves more attention. If it only works when I keep adjusting the evidence around it, that's useful to know too.

More than once I've gone back to something I was convinced mattered and found that it looked much less impressive a few months later.

I consider that a good outcome.

I'd rather discover that I was forcing a pattern than spend years protecting one because I became attached to it.

So far, time has been a better filter than confidence.
