# 1.0 Assessment Basis

Access to more accurate information does not necessarily increase developmental autonomy.

Where one population receives information from a source believed to possess substantially greater knowledge, prediction, or capability, the receiving population may reduce independent evaluation.

Assessment therefore distinguishes:

**knowledge acquisition**

from

**epistemic dependency**

---

# 2.0 Epistemic Dependency

For present assessment:

**epistemic dependency**

refers to a condition in which a population increasingly relies upon an external source to determine:

- what is true
- what requires attention
- what action is justified
- which uncertainty matters
- when revision is necessary

rather than maintaining sufficient internal capacity to evaluate those questions independently.

Dependency may occur even where the external source is accurate.

---

# 3.0 Accuracy Paradox

A highly accurate source may produce stronger dependency than an unreliable source.

As predictive success increases:

- source confidence may increase
- independent verification may decrease
- competing models may receive less attention
- disagreement may be interpreted as ignorance
- institutional authority may centralize

Thus:

**greater source accuracy**

can increase:

**dependency risk**

---

# 4.0 Verification Retention

External information should not eliminate internal verification where verification is possible.

Relevant developmental indicators include:

- replication
- independent observation
- competing model preservation
- source-independent prediction
- contradiction reporting
- correction after external-source error

A population that cannot disagree with a useful source remains vulnerable to that source.

---

# 5.0 Source Fallibility

No informational source should be modeled as incapable of error.

Possible causes of external-source error include:

- incomplete observation
- translation failure
- model mismatch
- scale mismatch
- temporal uncertainty
- incorrect boundary selection
- changing conditions

High capability does not eliminate fallibility.

---

# 6.0 Authority Capture

External-source information may be captured by internal actors.

Potential mechanisms include:

- restricted access
- selective disclosure
- false attribution
- interpretive monopoly
- institutional gatekeeping
- coercive enforcement
- political appropriation

Where access is unequal, epistemic hierarchy may emerge rapidly.

---

# 7.0 Verification Inequality

A claim may be independently testable by only a small portion of the population.

This creates separation between:

**those who can evaluate**

and

**those who must trust evaluators**

Such separation already exists within TP-3.

External interaction may increase it substantially.

---

# 8.0 Distributed Evaluation

Dependency risk decreases where evaluation remains distributed across:

- institutions
- disciplines
- regions
- populations
- methodological traditions
- competing explanatory models

Distributed disagreement may reduce decision speed.

It may increase error-detection capacity.

---

# 9.0 Disagreement Tolerance

Developmental autonomy requires tolerance for disagreement where evidence remains incomplete.

This does not require equal weighting of all claims.

It requires preservation of legitimate model competition until evidence discriminates sufficiently.

Suppression of disagreement by source status reduces epistemic resilience.

---

# 10.0 Contradiction Response

A useful external source may eventually produce information inconsistent with established TP-3 models.

Possible responses include:

- immediate acceptance
- immediate rejection
- reinterpretation
- independent testing
- model competition
- provisional integration

The most developmentally informative response preserves both:

**evidentiary discipline**

and

**capacity for revision**

---

# 11.0 Deference

Deference may be rational where:

- expertise difference is substantial
- verification cost is high
- consequence of delay is large
- prior source reliability is strong

Deference is not equivalent to dependency.

Relevant distinction:

**provisional reliance**

versus

**replacement of internal evaluation**

---

# 12.0 Dependency Escalation

Epistemic dependency may increase through repeated successful guidance.

Possible progression:

**external correction**

→

**habitual consultation**

→

**reduced independent prediction**

→

**institutional reliance**

→

**loss of autonomous model maintenance**

The transition may be gradual.

---

# 13.0 Instruction Frequency

Frequent intervention may prevent learning that would otherwise occur through:

- failure
- correction
- experimentation
- model competition
- institutional adaptation

Repeated successful rescue can therefore increase future vulnerability.

---

# 14.0 Error Ownership

A population that acts on external instruction may assign outcome responsibility to the source.

This can reduce internal consequence integration.

Relevant distinction:

**we chose under uncertainty**

versus

**we followed instruction**

The second may weaken model revision after failure.

---

# 15.0 External Disagreement

Where multiple external populations or sources provide conflicting information, a dependent population may lack mechanisms for evaluation.

Dependence on authority becomes unstable where authority is plural.

A population prepared for wider integration must therefore retain source-independent discrimination.

---

# 16.0 Knowledge Without Method

Information transferred without the reasoning structure that produced it may be difficult to evaluate.

This is especially relevant where:

- underlying models are inaccessible
- evidence cannot be reproduced
- assumptions are unknown
- causal boundaries differ
- translation is approximate

Correct conclusion without transferable method produces limited autonomy.

---

# 17.0 Method Transfer

Where external knowledge is introduced, lower dependency may result if emphasis remains on:

- method
- uncertainty
- model limits
- reproducibility
- causal relationships
- revision conditions

rather than:

- authority
- conclusion
- command
- certainty

Method transfer can itself increase capability and therefore remains subject to transition-risk assessment.

---

# 18.0 TP-3 Existing Pattern

TP-3 already demonstrates significant epistemic delegation.

Examples include reliance upon:

- technical experts
- scientific institutions
- political authorities
- religious authorities
- media systems
- automated systems
- algorithmic recommendation structures

Delegation is unavoidable in complex populations.

Dependency assessment therefore concerns not whether delegation exists, but whether independent correction remains possible.

---

# 19.0 Automated Authority

TP-3 increasingly relies upon systems whose internal reasoning may be inaccessible to most users.

This creates a useful pre-transition analogue.

Relevant questions include:

- Is output accepted because it is accurate or because it appears authoritative?
- Are errors independently detectable?
- Can users challenge the system?
- Does convenience reduce verification?
- Does repeated usefulness produce over-deference?

These behaviors may provide developmental information concerning future external-source interaction.

---

# 20.0 Contact Relevance

A technologically superior external source would produce a much stronger authority asymmetry than existing TP-3 institutions.

Therefore present behavior toward:

- expertise
- automation
- institutional authority
- inaccessible technical systems

may provide partial indicators.

Generalization remains uncertain.

---

# 21.0 Autonomy Condition

Contact readiness increases where a population can:

- accept useful external information
- retain skepticism
- preserve competing explanations
- verify where possible
- identify source error
- resist authority capture
- revise without dependence
- continue independent model formation

The objective is not distrust.

The objective is:

**reliance without surrender of evaluation**

---

# 22.0 Assessment Principle

A population prepared to receive knowledge from a more capable intelligence must remain capable of deciding when that intelligence is wrong.

---

# 23.0 TP-3 Assessment

Current indicators:

**independent verification capacity: high but uneven**

**institutional plurality: substantial**

**authority capture risk: substantial**

**source-status sensitivity: high**

**disagreement tolerance: variable**

**automated deference: increasing**

**developmental autonomy: partial**

**external-source dependency risk: elevated**

---

# 24.0 Status

**External information exposure: limited**

**Repeated authoritative instruction: not recommended**

**Method-oriented exposure: conditionally permissible**

**Independent verification capacity: preserve**

**Epistemic dependency risk: elevated**

Continue observation.
