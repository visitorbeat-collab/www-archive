CROSS REFERENCE

cross_reference_004

comparison: encounter / testimony / contact framework confidence: moderate

1.0Basis

incremental_contact_assessment_001 provides a framework for comparing Testimony 001, Testimony 002, and the Finder's encounter. The comparison is structural. It does not establish a common source.

2.0Shared Pattern

All three records include a strong control asymmetry, incomplete or absent explanation, unstable recall, and an interpretive effect that lasts longer than any factual information. None contains a stable message that can be verified independently.

These features fit many ordinary and extraordinary explanations. Their recurrence is worth comparing, but recurrence alone is not discrimination.

Distress also recurs. The incremental-contact model says distress need not be the objective; fear can emerge from asymmetry and loss of predictive control. That possibility weakens a simple equation between fear and hostile intent, but it does not make the encounters benign.

3.0Differences

Testimony 001 treats environmental imagery as warning-like. Testimony 002 is more concerned with choice under incomplete information. The Finder's account is the most procedural and retains relationships rather than a message. Those differences matter; the accounts should not be made identical merely because one framework can contain them.

The Finder account has the strongest overlap with the cognitive protocol: examination-like procedure, no stable semantic message, and long-term retention of relationships rather than facts. That makes it the most tempting match. It also makes selection bias especially important, because the Index was built around the Finder's own way of organizing the experience.

4.0Environmental Content

Environmental imagery may communicate a warning. It may also provide a useful problem space: interacting variables, delayed effects, uncertain boundaries, and consequences outside immediate perception. In that second reading, the environment is assessment structure rather than message content. The two readings are not mutually exclusive, and neither is established.

5.0Alternative Models

unrelated human experiences
culturally related interpretations
partially related events with different mechanisms
one interaction class with variable responses
convergence produced by the Index itself

Current evidence does not exclude any of these.

Cultural contamination could travel through prior encounter narratives, religious language, environmental discourse, media, or later contact among witnesses. The accounts do not need to be fabricated for their similarities to be partly learned. Memory and interpretation can converge while the people reporting them remain sincere.

6.0Limitation

Compatibility with a protocol does not show that the protocol was applied. The framework is broad, the accounts are ambiguous, and this comparison was made after the relevant materials were already inside the Index. Selection by the Finder remains a possible cause of the match.

The strongest shared relationship is therefore deliberately unspectacular: interaction under incomplete explanation produces long-duration interpretation. That formulation is weaker than “message” or “abduction protocol,” but it survives more of the available alternatives.

7.0Assessment

Structural compatibility: moderate
Common origin: unresolved
Semantic-message interpretation: weak
Diagnostic-interaction interpretation: plausible
Independent confirmation: absent

Three human records contain features compatible with constrained diagnostic interaction. That describes the comparison, not what actually occurred. Retain; do not upgrade source classification.
