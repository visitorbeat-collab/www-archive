HUMAN TESTIMONY / UNVERIFIED

testimony_003

witness: T-03 source reliability: unverified

1.0Preface

The witness describes an experience involving non-human figures, apparent environmental displacement, and repeated attempts to obtain clarification.

Unlike previous testimony, the witness does not describe receiving a clear warning, instruction, or symbolic message.

The defining feature of the account is the opposite:

the witness repeatedly expected an answer and did not receive one

2.0Witness Account

I remember asking questions. A lot of them, actually.

That's one of the few parts of the experience I feel reasonably certain about, although I couldn't tell you whether I was speaking out loud. I don't even know if speaking was possible where I was.

But I remember asking.

At first it was the obvious things. Where am I? Who are you? What is happening?

I kept expecting some kind of response.

What makes it difficult to explain is that I didn't feel ignored exactly.

I don't remember hearing language. There was no voice that I can recall, and definitely nothing like words appearing in my head.

But I had a very strong impression that the questions were understood.

And then just left unanswered.

I realize that's an interpretation, not something I can prove. At the time, though, I was completely convinced of it.

3.0Environment

There was something in front of me that I've described as a display, although I'm not sure that's the right word.

Calling it a screen makes it sound like an object sitting in front of me, and I don't remember it feeling separate from the space around me like that.

There were points or areas connected to other points.

Some became brighter. Some disappeared. Other parts changed when something somewhere else changed.

At first I thought I was looking at locations.

Then I decided they were choices.

Later I thought maybe they were events instead.

I kept changing my mind about what I was looking at, which is probably important because even now I can't give you an answer I'm comfortable with.

4.0First Interpretation

The clearest question I remember asking was:

Which one is right?

I wanted one of the paths — if they were paths — to be identified for me.

I wanted somebody to tell me which one I was supposed to follow.

Nothing happened.

I got frustrated pretty quickly.

I remember thinking that if whoever was there could show me something this complicated, then surely they could explain what I was supposed to do with it.

That seemed completely reasonable to me at the time.

No explanation came.

5.0Change

At some point I stopped trying to identify the correct option.

I don't remember deciding to do that. I think I just got tired of getting nowhere.

Instead I started watching what happened when one part changed.

That was when I began noticing relationships I had missed before.

A change in one place might affect something else much later. Some effects were immediate, some weren't. Things that looked separate turned out to be connected, while things I had assumed were important sometimes barely changed anything at all.

I became absorbed in trying to follow it.

And I remember realizing, sometime afterward, that I wasn't as afraid anymore.

I hadn't solved anything. I had just stopped demanding that someone tell me what the answer was.

I don't remember consciously deciding to stop asking questions.

I only noticed later that I had.

6.0Second Question

Later I asked something different.

I can't reconstruct the exact wording anymore, but it was something close to:

What do you want me to do?

Again, there was no answer.

For some reason that bothered me more than the earlier silence.

I had an incredibly strong feeling that they could have answered if they wanted to.

I can't support that with anything objective.

Maybe I was dreaming. Maybe I was alone. Maybe the sense that another intelligence was present was part of whatever the experience itself was.

Those possibilities are still available to me.

But if I'm describing the memory rather than what I think about it now, then in that moment I believed the lack of an answer was intentional.

7.0Emotional Response

I got angry.

Not panicked. It was different from the fear I'd had earlier.

I remember thinking:

If you know more than I do, why are you making me guess?

That thought has stayed with me longer than almost anything else from the experience.

There was no reassurance afterward.

No correction either.

I didn't get any sense of approval when I followed one relationship rather than another, and I don't remember anything that felt like punishment when I misunderstood.

That was part of what made the whole thing so frustrating. I kept expecting feedback because I assumed feedback was necessary.

Apparently it wasn't, or at least I didn't receive any.

8.0Return

My next clear memory is being somewhere familiar again.

I don't remember the transition.

I had a headache and this awful feeling that I had forgotten something important, although I couldn't have said what.

I wrote down a handful of phrases while the memory still felt close.

Most of them don't mean much to me now.

One of them does.

THE ANSWER CHANGES THE TEST

I don't remember writing that sentence.

I know that sounds convenient. I don't have a better explanation for it.

9.0Afterward

For several weeks afterward I was obsessed with figuring out what the experience meant.

I kept changing the subject of the supposed message.

At first I thought it was environmental. Then political. Then maybe personal.

I wondered whether I had been warned about something, whether I had been tested, or whether the entire thing had simply been a very elaborate dream.

Every explanation seemed to account for part of it.

None of them really accounted for all of it.

Eventually I got tired of trying to extract a message from the experience.

And once I stopped doing that, I started thinking more about something else.

Why had I been so desperate for whoever was there to tell me what everything meant?

That question was more uncomfortable than the others because it wasn't really about them anymore.

10.0Authority

I've generally trusted people who know more about something than I do.

Doctors, engineers, researchers. Anyone dealing with a system I don't understand very well.

That still seems reasonable to me.

But afterward I started noticing how easily I could move from:

they know more than I do

to:

therefore I should stop evaluating what they tell me

I don't think I had ever separated those two ideas before.

I do now.

11.0The Refusal

For years I told people, when I told anyone at all, that they refused to answer me.

I'm less comfortable with that wording now.

"Refused" makes it sound as though an answer belonged to me and somebody deliberately withheld it.

Maybe that's what happened.

But maybe there wasn't an answer of the kind I wanted.

Or maybe giving me one would have changed whatever was happening.

I have no way to distinguish those possibilities.

There is one interpretation I particularly dislike.

Maybe the important thing was what I did when nobody explained the situation for me.

That makes the memory feel much more clinical than the idea that I was being shown a message.

Unfortunately, it also fits the experience better.

12.0Long-Term Effect

I can't identify any new fact that the experience gave me.

I didn't come back knowing something I couldn't have known before. I didn't predict anything, receive technology, discover a religion, or become convinced that whoever was there was benevolent.

I'm not even completely certain there was a "whoever."

The lasting change was much smaller than that.

When someone presents themselves as an authority now, I tend to notice two questions where I used to notice only one.

What do they know?
Why should that determine what I think?

Those questions aren't opposites.

I can recognize that someone understands something better than I do without handing over the responsibility for deciding what I believe.

Before the experience, I'm not sure I understood that distinction very well.

13.0Current Interpretation

If the experience had a purpose, I don't think anymore that it was simply to give me an answer.

If anything, I came away less comfortable with answers that arrive already attached to authority.

That could be coincidence.

It could be ordinary psychology. It could also be a lesson I've imposed on the memory over the years because it makes the experience easier to organize.

I genuinely can't distinguish between those possibilities.

What I do remember is wanting an explanation more than almost anything else while it was happening.

And not getting one.

Whatever else I've added since then, that part has never really changed.
