# Annotation

The phrase:

**THE ANSWER CHANGES THE TEST**

is exactly the kind of thing I should distrust.

Not because it is obviously false.

Because it is elegant.

---

A compact sentence that appears to explain several unrelated observations at once is unusually easy to overvalue.

It feels like resolution.

That feeling is not evidence.

---

The phrase appears compatible with:

- cognitive assessment
- withheld explanation
- developmental autonomy
- source-authority effects
- diagnostic contamination

That compatibility is real.

Its evidentiary significance remains uncertain.

---

There are at least four separate questions:

1. Did the witness actually write the phrase when reported?
2. Did the phrase originate during the experience?
3. Does it describe the structure of the experience accurately?
4. Does that structure share an origin with the recovered material?

A positive answer to one does not establish the others.

---

I am most concerned about the third question.

The phrase is so efficient that it may compress several uncertain relationships into one apparently obvious principle.

That can hide ambiguity rather than resolve it.

---

I have seen this problem elsewhere in the Index.

A useful sentence becomes a label.

The label becomes a category.

The category begins to look like an explanation.

Eventually the explanation becomes difficult to question because everything has been organized beneath it.

That is a failure mode.

---

So I am retaining the phrase without promoting it.

It remains witness material.

It is not recovered terminology.

It is not a key.

It is not a conclusion.

---

There is another reason to be careful.

I want it to be important.

That alone increases the burden.
