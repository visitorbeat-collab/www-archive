# 1.0 Purpose

Establish standardized observational categories for monitored biospheres.

Ensure consistent interpretation across independent observation groups.

Minimize ambiguity arising from localized conceptual frameworks.

---

# 2.0 Primary Unit

The primary observational unit is not the individual organism.

The primary observational unit is the interacting system.

Individual organisms should only be evaluated within the context of system behavior.

---

# 3.0 System Hierarchy

**Level 1 — Planetary System**

**Level 2 — Biosphere**

**Level 3 — Ecological Network**

**Level 4 — Technological Population (where present)**

**Level 5 — Individual Organism**

Evaluation should proceed from higher-order relationships toward lower-order observations.

Reverse evaluation frequently produces misleading conclusions.

---

# 4.0 Observation Priority

Observation should prioritize:

1. Stability
2. Adaptation
3. Information Transfer
4. Resource Distribution
5. Cognitive Development
6. Technological Capability
7. Individual Behavior

The ordering is significant.

Technological capability should not be interpreted independently of the systems within which that capability operates.

---

# 5.0 Interpretation

Observation is descriptive.

Interpretation is predictive.

Interpretation must never precede sufficient observation.

Prediction confidence decreases in proportion to unresolved variance.

Classification should remain provisional where multiple interpretations remain consistent with available observation.

---

# 6.0 Intervention

Intervention should not occur unless observational objectives cannot otherwise be achieved.

Intervention introduces distortion.

Distortion reduces observational value.

All interventions require subsequent behavioral assessment.

The consequences of intervention must themselves become observational data.

---

# 7.0 Emergent Technological Populations

Technological populations exhibit accelerated influence over system stability.

Evaluation should prioritize:

- long-term adaptive capacity
- resource utilization efficiency
- response to internally generated existential risk
- capacity for cooperative revision of maladaptive behavior
- effect upon supporting ecological systems
- capacity to recognize consequences beyond immediate population interests

Observed intelligence should not be inferred from technological capability alone.

Increasing capability increases both constructive and destructive potential.

Capability therefore provides insufficient evidence of developmental maturity.

---

# 8.0 System Boundary

Observed instability should be evaluated at multiple system scales.

A behavior may produce local stability while generating instability within a larger system.

Apparent externalities frequently indicate an incorrectly defined system boundary.

Where interpretation changes substantially following expansion of the observational boundary, the broader model should receive increased consideration.

---

# 9.0 Developmental Persistence

The emergence of technological intelligence does not demonstrate developmental persistence.

Persistence requires the continued maintenance of conditions compatible with:

- biological continuity
- ecological function
- cognitive development
- social adaptation
- continued self-correction

Populations unable to maintain the systems required for their own persistence should be classified as developmentally unstable regardless of technological capability.

---

# 10.0 Warning

Repeated observational error has resulted from assigning disproportionate significance to individual organisms.

Individual intelligence should not be assumed to predict population-level behavior.

Population-level behavior should not be assumed to predict biosphere-level stability.

Technological capability should not be assumed to predict developmental maturity.

System-level behavior remains the preferred predictive model.

---

# 11.0 Revision Notice

Conceptual translation into localized symbolic systems necessarily introduces classification loss.

Perfect equivalence is not expected.

Interpretation accuracy should therefore be evaluated independently of translation fidelity.

Terms including:

- intelligence
- population
- stability
- development
- system
- observation

represent approximate conceptual mappings rather than direct linguistic equivalents.
